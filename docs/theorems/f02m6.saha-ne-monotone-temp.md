### 1.2 Definitions used

$$
U(T)=\sum_{k\in\iota} g_k e^{-E_k/(k_B T)},\qquad
U_1(T)=\sum_{k\in\kappa} g'_k e^{-E'_k/(k_B T)}
$$

where $U(T)$ and $U_1(T)$ are the lower- and upper-stage partition functions
(`partitionFunction`).

$$
\theta(T)=\frac{2\pi m_e k_B T}{h^2}
$$

is the thermal bracket (`thermalBracket`).

$$
S(T)=2\,\frac{U_1(T)}{U(T)}\,\theta(T)^{3/2}\,\exp(-\chi/(k_B T))
$$

is the ideal Saha factor (`sahaFactor`).

$$
n_e(S,N)=\frac{-S+\sqrt{S^2+4SN}}{2}
$$

is the positive root of $n^2=S(N-n)$ (`sahaEquilibriumNe`). For $S>0$ and $N>0$ this is the
unique positive root; the Lean definition is the closed form for all real arguments, which never
matters here because $S(T)>0$ (H1, H3, nonempty level sets) and $N_{\rm tot}>0$ (H6). Likewise
$U(T)>0$, so the ratio in $S(T)$ never meets Lean's $a/0=0$ convention.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hkB`, `hme`, `hh`) — $k_B>0$, $m_e>0$, and $h>0$.
- **H2** (`hchi`) — $\chi\ge0$.
- **H3** (`hgZ`, `hgZ1`) — $g_k>0$ for every $k\in\iota$ and $g'_k>0$ for every $k\in\kappa$.
- **H4** (`hEZ`, `hEZ1`) — $E_k\ge0$ for every $k\in\iota$ and $E'_k\ge0$ for every $k\in\kappa$.
- **H5** (`hEχ`) — $E_k\le\chi$ for every $k\in\iota$.
- **H6** (`hN`) — $N_{\rm tot}>0$.

### 1.4 Conclusion

The map $T\mapsto n_e(S(T),N_{\rm tot})$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\infty)$. Equivalently,
for all $T_1,T_2\in(0,\infty)$ with $T_1 \lt T_2$,

$$
n_e(S(T_1),N_{\rm tot}) \lt n_e(S(T_2),N_{\rm tot}).
$$

## 2. Physical meaning

In the reduced LTE core, and provided the supplied lower-stage level list stops at or below
$\chi$ (H5), the theorem formalizes the qualitative expectation that a hotter plasma at fixed
total elemental density has a larger self-consistent electron density. H5 is load-bearing: it is
the hypothesis under which $S(T)$ is strictly increasing [proved:
CflibsFormal.sahaFactor_strictMonoOn_temp], and untruncated level lists are not covered. The
conclusion is about the model's equilibrium root: it is strictly increasing (not merely
non-decreasing); the theorem states no bound or limit.

## 3. Scope and validity

The generated scope-tags line (own relation tag to published tag, with the `via` definition) will
be rendered here by tooling; this card does not retype it.

- **Idealizations encoded:** one element, two stages (neutral and singly ionized), electrons
  supplied only by this element's single ionization ($n_e=n_{\rm II}$), fixed
  $N_{\rm tot}=n_{\rm I}+n_{\rm II}$, finite nonempty level lists, a lower-stage list truncated at
  or below $\chi$ (H5; D17's baseline policy, a fixed cutoff at the unperturbed ionization energy,
  produces such a list, but the theorem assumes no policy), and the ideal Saha factor with no
  ionization-potential depression and a temperature-independent `chi`.
- **What it does NOT claim:**
  - It does not cover a lowered, state-dependent ionization potential of the form
    $\chi-\Delta\chi(n_e,T)$, because `chi` is a fixed real constant and `sahaEquilibriumNe`
    solves the stated closed-form equation.
  - It does not assert that raw level databases satisfy `hEχ`; `hEχ` is a cutoff obligation on the
    supplied lower-stage list [derivation, unchecked].
  - It does not provide a quantitative ionization fraction, an error bound, or any improvement in
    measurement accuracy.
  - It does not extend to multi-element coupling, time dependence, or non-LTE kinetics.
  - It holds $N_{\rm tot}$ fixed; a fixed-pressure closure, in which the total density is itself a
    function of $T$ and $n_e$, is outside the statement (for example the multi-element,
    three-stage pressure-balance routine `compute_equilibrium_ne` in CF-LIBS-improved's
    `cflibs/validation/round_trip.py`).
  - It does not claim the conclusion fails when H5 is violated; necessity of H5 is not asserted.
  - It is about the model's equilibrium root, not about an electron density measured from Stark
    widths.
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** H2 (`hchi`) and the lower-stage half of H4 (`hEZ`) are
  not load-bearing (see §4). Whether H5 or `hEZ1` can be weakened is not asserted. Non-vacuity is
  witnessed in
  `CflibsFormal/SahaStability.lean` by two examples: a `Fin 1` instance with unit constants,
  `chi = 1`, ground-state-only lists, `Ntot = 1`, and temperatures satisfying $1<2$; and a
  `Fin 2` instance with `EZ = ![0, 9/10]`, `chi = 1`, `gZ1` constantly `2`,
  `EZ1 = ![0, 1/2]`, `Ntot = 5`, and temperatures satisfying $1<3$.

## 4. Proof idea

The proof composes three landed facts. For $0 \lt T_1 \lt T_2$, `sahaFactor_pos` proves
$0 \lt S(T_1)$ and $0 \lt S(T_2)$. `sahaFactor_strictMonoOn_temp` proves $S(T_1) \lt S(T_2)$.
`sahaEquilibriumNe_strictMono_S` proves $S\mapsto n_e(S,N_{\rm tot})$ is strictly increasing on
$(0,\infty)$. The Lean body therefore applies the composition directly. `hchi` and `hEZ` are
passed to `sahaFactor_strictMonoOn_temp`, which binds them as `_hchi` and `_hEZ` and does not use
them (both Lean docstrings say they are carried for API parity); `sahaFactor_pos` needs neither,
so neither is load-bearing.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** No pipeline code relies on this theorem. CF-LIBS-improved's
  `cflibs/inversion/physics/certificates.py` defines `_saha_equilibrium_ne`, documented as a
  mirror of the definition `sahaEquilibriumNe` and used by `saha_iter_certificate`; it takes the
  Saha factor as an input and does not vary temperature.
- **Proposed use:** A component in CF-LIBS-improved that evaluates the reduced Saha equilibrium
  density could cite this theorem as the proved monotonicity invariant for that composition, for
  example to order temperature samples or to flag non-monotone evaluations of the same reduced
  model [derivation, unchecked].
- **What it would change:** It would change rigor, not accuracy: the monotonicity of the reduced
  model's equilibrium electron density would be a kernel-checked fact rather than an assumed
  physical expectation. It does not make any measurement more accurate.
- **How it would be validated:** Primary validation is Lean kernel replay. For the proposed use:
  a companion test evaluates the Python Saha factor and `_saha_equilibrium_ne` on a temperature
  grid, with constants and a lower-stage list truncated at or below χ that satisfy H1-H6, and
  asserts strict increase. Falsification arm: any adjacent grid pair $T_1 \lt T_2$ whose evaluated
  root does not increase beyond float tolerance shows the Python model departs from the Lean
  definitions (or that the list violates H5).
