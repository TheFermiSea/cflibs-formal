### 1.2 Definitions used

- `responseFactor` — the line response factor of the classic reader: the product of the chosen-line degeneracy, chosen-line Einstein coefficient and Boltzmann factor, divided by the partition function.

- `composition` — the number fraction of a species in a finite density vector: the species density divided by the total density.
- `totalDensity` — the finite sum of all species densities, used by `composition`.

## 2. Physical meaning

The classic CF-LIBS reader converts a measured line intensity into a density by dividing by the calibration factor times a line response factor. Tognoni 2010 identifies atomic-parameter uncertainty, chiefly in transition probabilities, as a dominant CF-LIBS error source [cited: Tognoni 2010]. In the situation formalized here, each species emits with its true atomic data at a temperature the reader knows, with one calibration factor shared by emission and reading, and the analyst reads the chosen line with wrong atomic data. Collapsing the per-symbol errors in g, E, A and U into a single relative response-factor bound δ, shared by all species, is this formalization's own reduction, not a literature claim.

The exact aliasing identity for the classic reader says that the recovered density for species `t` is the true density multiplied by the ratio of the true response factor to the analyst response factor. [proved: CflibsFormal.classicDensity_aliasing] H9 bounds that ratio two-sidedly. After closure, the recovered number fraction differs from the true number fraction by at most a constant times the true number fraction.

The constant is abundance-scaled: the bound is proportional to the true fraction of the species being reported. For example, with delta equal to 0.05, the constant is 2/19, approximately 0.105; a species whose true fraction is 0.005 is bounded by approximately 5.26e-4. [derivation, unchecked] This is the sense in which the result is useful for minor elements: the absolute error bound shrinks with the true abundance.

## 3. Scope and validity

The generated tags line will be inserted here by `gen_cards.py`.

- **Idealizations encoded:** optically thin LTE emission through the line-intensity model, a classic reader at known temperature, a common calibration factor, no self-absorption, no matrix effects, no measurement noise, and a lumped per-species relative response-factor error rather than separate bounds on degeneracy, energy, Einstein coefficient and partition-function errors; one ionization stage per species (a single partition function, no Saha coupling); a single chosen line u s per species; one bound δ shared by all species.
- **What it does NOT claim:** it does not claim that the individual atomic-data symbols are each off by at most delta; only the combined response factor is bounded. It does not claim that additive density errors are bounded by the same constant; additive errors can amplify minor-element errors. One instance is `composition_additive_error_amplifies_minor`, landed in `AtomicDataPerturbation` after this card and not a member of it: an error of 1/100 of the total density on each of two species `N = (99, 1)` gives the minor a relative composition error of 49/51, above the multiplicative bound 2η/(1 − η) at η = 1/100. It is one numerical instance; no general additive bound or amplification law is stated. It does not claim the bound is tight in Lean; the near-tightness discussion is an analytic construction. It does not claim a mass-fraction bound, an Aitchison-distance bound, an oxide-closure bound, or a bound under unknown temperature or calibration error. It does not claim that the classic reader is accurate or that the bound improves measured concentrations; it characterizes error transfer. It does not bound the error in terms of the recovered fraction Ĉ_s; the right-hand side uses the true fraction C_s, and converting it into an observable tolerance takes an extra step that is not landed.
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** a two-species, one-line witness makes the hypotheses satisfiable and the constant nearly tight. Take two species, one line, true and analyst degeneracies equal to 1, all energies equal to 0, true chosen-line Einstein coefficients equal to 1, analyst chosen-line Einstein coefficients equal to 1 minus delta for the target species and 1 plus delta for the other species, and true densities epsilon and 1 with epsilon positive. Then H1 through H9 hold for 0 less than or equal to delta less than 1, and as epsilon tends to 0 the relative error of the target species approaches 2*delta/(1-delta). [derivation, unchecked] A discrete Lean-checked instance at delta = 1/20 (two species, one line, N = (1,1), recovered C0 = 21/41 against true C0 = 1/2, hpert tight for the target species) is checked in [scratch: evidence/ft07.aitchison-atomic-data-error-transfer/ft07_witness.lean].

## 4. Proof idea

The proof uses the exact aliasing identity for the classic reader, `classicDensity_aliasing`, to rewrite the recovered density as the true density times the true response factor divided by the analyst response factor. [proved: CflibsFormal.classicDensity_aliasing]

It first proves positivity of the true and analyst response factors by unfolding `responseFactor` and using `boltzmannFactor_pos` and `partitionFunction_pos`. H9 is then converted into two-sided ratio bounds between the true density and the recovered density for every species, using `abs_le`, `le_div_iff₀`, `div_le_iff₀`, `mul_div_assoc'` and `nlinarith`.

Finally, the proof applies the closure helper `comp_rel_ratio`, which turns two-sided ratio bounds of the form 1-minus-delta times recovered density less than or equal to true density less than or equal to 1-plus-delta times recovered density into the relative composition bound with constant 2*delta/(1-delta).

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** the theorem is landed in `CflibsFormal`. This card records no pipeline wiring, and no private pipeline anchor is named here.
- **Proposed use:** use the theorem as a relative error-transfer check for the classic CF-LIBS reader when atomic-data uncertainty can be summarized by a per-species relative response-factor bound. It can support diagnostics that compare recovered number fractions against a relative tolerance scaled by the true or reference fraction.
- **What it would change:** it changes the rigor of the error analysis, not the measured values. It replaces an informal statement that small atomic-data error gives small fraction error with a formal abundance-scaled bound. It does not pitch the result as improving measurement accuracy.
- **How it would be validated:** the statement is falsifiable in exact arithmetic. A falsification arm is a dataset or synthetic case in which H1 through H9 are verified, including H9 for every species, but the recovered number fraction violates the displayed inequality. Such a violation would indicate an error in the implementation, in the verification of H9, or in the theorem environment. A control arm is the delta-equal-to-zero case, where the bound becomes zero and the recovered and true number fractions should match exactly in the model.
