## 2. Physical meaning

In the CF-LIBS setting, `CflibsFormal.partitionFunction` is the finite Boltzmann sum used to normalize level populations for a species, and `CflibsFormal.partitionFunctionCut` is the same sum after a fixed sharp energy cutoff keeps levels strictly below `cut` [derivation, unchecked]. Read physically, the headline theorem says that the factor by which truncation changes the normalization is strictly increasing with temperature whenever the cutoff actually keeps some levels and drops some levels [derivation, unchecked]. The Lean content itself is the strict monotonicity of the ratio on positive temperatures. The theorem does not encode a physical cutoff policy, an electron-density dependence, or a numerical bias size [derivation, unchecked].

## 3. Scope and validity

The generated Facts block will supply the scope tags; this draft does not retype them.

- **Idealizations encoded:** finite level set; real-valued energies and degeneracies; Boltzmann exponential factor; fixed cutoff independent of temperature and electron density; no line-shape, plasma-state, or ionization-potential-depression model; no `n_e`-dependent cutoff.
- **What it does NOT claim:** The following scope exclusions are card-author readings of the statement [derivation, unchecked]:
  - It does not prove `Set.InjOn` for the ratio on `Set.Ioi 0`.
  - It does not prove the two-temperature non-absorbability corollary that no single real `c` satisfies both `U(T1) = c * U_cut(T1)` and `U(T2) = c * U_cut(T2)` for distinct positive temperatures.
  - It does not prove the FT-05 (i) tail bound $0 \le U - U_{cut} \le (\sum_{cut \le E_k} g_k)\, e^{-cut/(k_B T)}$. That bound is a separate declaration, `partitionFunction_sub_cut_bounds` in `CflibsFormal/Boltzmann.lean`, landed after this card and not a member of it.
  - It does not prove FT-05 (iv) (`population_cut_consistent`, the species-common bias $U_{cut}/U$ when the numerator and $U$ use mismatched level lists) or FT-05 (v) (`sharpCutoff_discontinuous`). Neither is stated in `CflibsFormal/`. D17's retirement of the $n_e$-dependent cutoff rests on the research memo that `docs/decisions.md` cites, not on a landed theorem.
  - It does not cover a cutoff that changes with temperature: `cut` is bound outside the function of `T`. A Debye–Hückel-lowered cutoff $\chi - \Delta\chi(n_e, T)$ moves with `T` and falls outside the statement.
  - It gives no magnitude: it bounds neither the ratio nor how fast it grows.
  - It does not claim that a real pipeline's `gA` corrections fail, that a particular cutoff is physically correct, or that measurement accuracy improves.
  - It does not cover the case with no dropped levels, where the ratio is identically 1, or the case with no kept levels, where the denominator is zero and Lean real division gives the junk value 0; both cases are excluded by `hkeep` and `hdrop`.
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** A concrete satisfiable instance is `ι := Fin 2`, `g 0 = g 1 = 1`, `E 0 = 0`, `E 1 = 1`, `cut = 1/2`, and `kB = 1`; then level 0 is kept and level 1 is dropped [derivation, unchecked]. The assumptions `hkeep` and `hdrop` are load-bearing: without `hdrop` the ratio is identically 1, and without `hkeep` the truncated denominator is zero [derivation, unchecked]. `hkB` is load-bearing: at kB = 0, Lean's x/0 = 0 makes every factor 1 and the ratio constant. `hg` is load-bearing: a zero weight on every dropped level makes the ratio identically 1. On this witness, the `hkB` counterexample replays and closes in Lean [scratch: evidence/ft05.partition-function-cutoff-ratio/hkB_counterexample.lean].

## 4. Proof idea

The proof uses `CflibsFormal.partitionFunctionCut_pos` to show that the truncated sums at both temperatures are positive, and `CflibsFormal.partitionFunction_eq_cut_add_tail` to write the full sum as the kept sum plus the dropped tail. It then uses `div_lt_div_iff₀` to cross-multiply the ratio inequality. The crux is the private helper `bf_cross_lt`, which proves the termwise Boltzmann-factor inequality for one kept level and one dropped level. The proof expands products with `Finset.sum_mul_sum`, applies `Finset.sum_lt_sum_of_nonempty` on the dropped and kept filters, and uses `Finset.mem_filter` to recover the kept and dropped energy inequalities. The exponent comparison uses `Real.exp_lt_exp`, `Real.exp_add`, `one_div_lt_one_div_of_lt`, `mul_lt_mul_of_pos_left`, `ring`, and `nlinarith`.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** `partitionFunctionCut` and `cutRatio_strictMonoOn_temp` are not named by any CF-LIBS-improved code; `status.pipeline` is `none`. CF-LIBS-improved already computes the same shape of truncated sum, though. `cflibs/plasma/partition.py#direct_sum_partition_function` keeps levels strictly below the ionization potential, the same strict keep convention as the card. With `n_e` omitted this is the fixed cutoff of `partitionFunctionCut` at `cut = IP` — D17's baseline — apart from a floor at 1 and a low-T shortcut. With `n_e` supplied, the Debye–Hückel lowering depends on `n_e` and `T`, so the cutoff moves with `T` and this theorem does not apply. `cflibs/plasma/saha_boltzmann.py#_ipd_max_energy_ev` is the `n_e`-dependent cutoff that D17 retires. A search for the theorem name in CF-LIBS-improved returns nothing.
- **Proposed use:** For a fixed sharp cutoff baseline of the kind selected by D17, a level-cutoff policy module could use this theorem as an invariant that the truncation correction factor is strictly temperature-dependent whenever the cutoff actually removes and keeps levels. This is a proposal, not a pipeline fact [derivation, unchecked].
- **What it would change:** It would add a proved monotonicity invariant to the formal specification of fixed-cutoff partition functions. It changes rigor, not measurement accuracy; it does not assert that any numerical estimate improves.
- **How it would be validated:** On a temperature grid inside the LIBS band, evaluate the pipeline's fixed-cutoff direct sum (no IPD) and the untruncated sum over the same level table. For every species with at least one level on each side of the cutoff, check that the ratio is strictly increasing. Falsification arm: a non-increasing step would show that the implementation differs from `partitionFunctionCut` at a fixed `cut` (a T-dependent cutoff, an active clamp, or mismatched level lists), not that the theorem is wrong.
