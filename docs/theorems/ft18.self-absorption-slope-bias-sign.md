## 1. Statement

All quantities are bare reals in the repository's dimensionless core. The statement is about a
finite set of spectral lines indexed by a type $\iota$.

### 1.1 Symbols

| Symbol | Lean name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $\iota$ | `ι` | finite, nonempty index set of spectral lines |
| $E_k$ | `E` | upper-level energy of line $k$ |
| $y_k$ | `y` | optically thin Boltzmann-plot ordinate of line $k$ |
| $\tau_k$ | `τ` | optical depth of line $k$ |
| $k,i,j$ | `k`,`i`,`j` | line indices used in the hypotheses and the self-absorbed ordinate |
| $\operatorname{SA}$ | `selfAbsorptionFactor` | flat-profile slab self-absorption factor |
| $\operatorname{olsSlope}$ | `olsSlope` | unweighted ordinary-least-squares slope |

### 1.2 Definitions used

- `mean` — arithmetic mean over the finite index type:

$$
\operatorname{mean}(f)=\frac{\sum_{k\in\iota} f_k}{\#\iota}.
$$

- `olsSlope` — unweighted OLS slope of ordinates $y$ against energies $E$:

$$
\operatorname{olsSlope}(E,y)=
\frac{\sum_{k\in\iota}(E_k-\operatorname{mean}(E))(y_k-\operatorname{mean}(y))}
{\sum_{k\in\iota}(E_k-\operatorname{mean}(E))^2}.
$$

- `selfAbsorptionFactor` — flat-profile slab escape factor:

$$
\operatorname{SA}(\tau)=
\begin{cases}
1, & \tau=0,\\
\dfrac{1-\exp(-\tau)}{\tau}, & \tau\neq 0.
\end{cases}
$$

Under H1, only the positive branch is used, and $\operatorname{SA}(\tau_k)>0$ [proved: CflibsFormal.selfAbsorptionFactor_pos].

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hτ`) — $0 \lt \tau_k$ for every $k\in\iota$.
- **H2** (`hanti`) — for all $i,j\in\iota$, if $E_i \lt E_j$ then $\tau_j \le \tau_i$.

### 1.4 Conclusion

For every finite nonempty index type $\iota$ and functions $E,y,\tau:\iota\to\mathbb R$
satisfying H1 and H2,

$$
\operatorname{olsSlope}(E,y)
\le
\operatorname{olsSlope}\left(E, k \mapsto y_k + \log\bigl(\operatorname{SA}(\tau_k)\bigr)\right).
$$

### 1.5 Lean correspondence

| Binder | Label | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `ι`, `[Fintype ι]`, `[Nonempty ι]` | V1 | The two anonymous catalog binders `_` are the instance arguments; they are not hypotheses. `[Nonempty ι]` is not logically needed (both slopes are $0$ with no lines) — it is carried for `olsSlope_eq_centered` |
| `E`, `y`, `τ` | V2 | Functions `ι → ℝ`, implicit `{E y τ : ι → ℝ}`; not hypotheses |
| `hτ` | H1 | strict positivity `0 < τ k`; it keeps `selfAbsorptionFactor (τ k)` positive, so `Real.log` is monotone there |
| `hanti` | H2 | strict energy antecedent $E_i \lt E_j$ and non-strict optical-depth consequent $\tau_j \le \tau_i$; equal-energy pairs are unrestricted |

Reading conventions:

- `olsSlope` uses Lean's totalized real division. If
  $\sum_k (E_k-\operatorname{mean}(E))^2=0$, both slopes in the conclusion are $0$ [derivation,
  unchecked].
- `selfAbsorptionFactor` is defined with an `if tau = 0` branch; under H1 every $\tau_k \ne 0$,
  so only the $(1-e^{-\tau})/\tau$ branch is evaluated.
- `Real.log` is the natural logarithm.

## 2. Physical meaning

In the intended Boltzmann-plot reading, $y_k$ is an optically thin ordinate and the measured
ordinate after self-absorption is $y_k+\log\operatorname{SA}(\tau_k)$ [derivation, unchecked].
Because $\operatorname{SA}$ is strictly decreasing on positive optical depth [proved:
CflibsFormal.selfAbsorptionFactor_strictAntiOn], the correction $\log\operatorname{SA}(\tau_k)$
is nondecreasing along $E$ under H2, so it has nonnegative centred covariance with $E$ (zero,
e.g., when all $\tau_k$ are equal) and cannot lower the unweighted OLS slope.

If the thin slope is negative, as in a single-temperature Boltzmann plot with slope
$-1/(k_B T)$, the inequality says only that the self-absorbed slope is not more negative than
$-1/(k_B T)$: it may equal it, lie between it and $0$, or reach or cross $0$, in which case no
positive apparent temperature is defined [derivation, unchecked]. The apparent-temperature
consequence is not part of this statement: it requires an additional negative-slope hypothesis.
It is `apparentT_ge_of_selfAbsorbed_slope`, landed in `SelfAbsorption` after this card and not a
member of it.

## 3. Scope and validity

The generated Facts block supplies the own relation tag and the published tag; this prose does
not retype those tags.

- **Idealizations encoded:** flat-profile slab escape factor applied to integrated line
  intensity; unweighted OLS at a single temperature; bare real-valued line parameters; no line
  shape, no spatial profile, no weighted fit, and no statistical noise model [derivation,
  unchecked].
- **What it does NOT claim:** it does not claim that real line sets satisfy H2; it does not
  claim the apparent temperature is overestimated; it says nothing about line sets where
  $\tau$ increases with $E$ — there the slope can decrease (the two-line witness
  `olsSlope_selfAbsorbed_lt_of_tau_increasing`, landed in `SelfAbsorption` after this card and
  not a member of it); it does not claim the result extends to weighted fits or
  profile-integrated escape factors; it does not settle any literature dispute about sign; it
  does not claim strict increase (equality holds, e.g., when all $\tau_k$ or all $E_k$ are
  equal); it gives no bound on the size of the slope shift; it says nothing about the OLS
  intercept, which carries the composition; and it says nothing about a fit corrected with an
  estimated $\hat\tau$.
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** H2 is stated only for strictly ordered energy pairs,
  so lines sharing an upper level may have different optical depths [derivation, unchecked]. A
  non-vacuous witness is $\iota=\mathrm{Fin}\,2$, $E_0=0$, $E_1=1$, $\tau_0=2$, $\tau_1=1$, and
  $y_0=y_1=0$; H1 and H2 hold, and the energy denominator is nonzero [derivation, unchecked]. A
  non-degenerate witness with a nonzero thin slope was executed against the landed statement —
  $\iota=\mathrm{Fin}\,2$, $E=(0,1)$, $\tau=(2,1)$, $y=(0,-1)$, thin slope $-1$ — recorded in
  `docs/archive/openprover/staging/2026-09-24/_workflow_result.json`. H1 is the physical domain;
  mathematically it is not needed, since $\operatorname{SA}(\tau)=\int_0^1 e^{-\tau s}\,ds$ is
  positive and strictly decreasing on all of $\mathbb R$ [derivation, unchecked]. The statement
  holds for arbitrary $y$; a single-temperature line is part of the reading, not a hypothesis.

## 4. Proof idea

The proof source uses the following lemmas and helpers:

- `olsSlope_add'` — the OLS slope is additive in the ordinates.
- `olsSlope_eq_centered` — rewrites the slope as a centred covariance divided by the energy
  variance sum.
- `logSA_mono` — derives monotonicity of $k \mapsto \log\operatorname{SA}(\tau_k)$ along $E$
  from H1 and H2, via mathlib's `Real.log_le_log`.
- `selfAbsorptionFactor_pos` — positivity of the slab factor for nonnegative optical depth.
- `selfAbsorptionFactor_strictAntiOn` — strict antitonicity of the slab factor on positive
  optical depth.
- `centered_cov_nonneg` — the centred covariance of similarly ordered sequences is nonnegative,
  via mathlib's Chebyshev sum inequality `Monovary.sum_mul_sum_le_card_mul_sum`.
- `div_nonneg`, `Finset.sum_nonneg`, `sq_nonneg`, and `linarith` — assemble the final
  inequality.

The mathematical route is: additivity of the slope reduces the claim to showing that the slope
of the self-absorption correction is nonnegative; that correction is nondecreasing in $E$ under
H2; similarly ordered sequences have nonnegative centred covariance; and the energy variance
denominator is nonnegative [derivation, unchecked].

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** No CF-LIBS-improved code references this theorem. The pipeline's
  Boltzmann fit, `cflibs/inversion/physics/boltzmann.py#BoltzmannPlotFitter`, is weighted and
  sigma-clipped by default, so FT-18's unweighted statement over a fixed line set is a
  reduction of it; the flat-profile factor appears as
  `cflibs/inversion/physics/self_absorption.py#_escape_factor`; resonance lines, the lines H2
  expects to be most absorbed, are kept by default in
  `cflibs/inversion/physics/line_selection.py#LineSelector`.
- **Proposed use:** as a conditional sign invariant for unweighted Boltzmann-plot slope when
  diagnosing or applying self-absorption corrections; given per-line $\tau$ estimates (e.g.
  doublet-ratio or curve-of-growth), check whether they satisfy H2 before reading the
  slope-bias sign [derivation, unchecked].
- **What it would change:** rigor only. It makes the conditional direction of the slope bias
  explicit and gives a falsifiable invariant for the reduced model. It does not improve
  measurement accuracy [derivation, unchecked].
- **How it would be validated:** validate on synthetic line sets with known optical-depth
  ordering, and on real line sets only after checking H1 and H2. Falsification: a line set with
  $\tau$ certified to satisfy H1–H2 whose self-absorbed unweighted OLS slope is below the thin
  slope would show that the pipeline ordinates or fit differ from the model (weights, clipping,
  non-flat profile), not that the proved statement fails.

## 6. Status and remaining work

- No claim is made that real CF-LIBS line sets satisfy H2; the $\tau$-versus-$E$ ordering is an
  empirical per-line-set check before applying the theorem.
- No CF-LIBS-improved code references this theorem; the default Boltzmann fit is weighted and
  sigma-clipped, so applying FT-18 there needs a weighted fixed-line-set analogue or an
  unweighted diagnostic fit.

Landed after this card, not members of it (both in `SelfAbsorption`): the apparent-temperature
corollary `apparentT_ge_of_selfAbsorbed_slope` (thin slope $-1/(k_B T)$, $0 \lt k_B$ and a negative
self-absorbed slope give apparent temperature $\ge T$; $0 \lt T$ is derived, not assumed), and the
reverse-ordering witness `olsSlope_selfAbsorbed_lt_of_tau_increasing` ($\tau$ increasing with $E$
lowers the slope strictly on a two-line instance).
