### 1.2 Definitions used

$$
\mathcal B(E;k_B,T)=e^{-E/(k_B T)}\qquad(\texttt{boltzmannFactor})
$$

$$
L(k_B,T,N,F_{\rm cal},U_s,\ell)
=F_{\rm cal}\,A_\ell\,\frac{N\,g_\ell\,\mathcal B(E_\ell;k_B,T)}{U_s}
\qquad(\texttt{lineIntensityPerU})
$$

Here $g_\ell$, $E_\ell$, and $A_\ell$ are the values of the fixed line functions `g`, `E`, and `A` at line $\ell$.

$$
C_s=\frac{I_s/J_s}{\sum_t I_t/J_t}
\qquad(\texttt{closureEstimate})
$$

$$
Q_{R,n_e}=\frac{\sum_s I_s R_s/J_s}{n_e}
\qquad(\texttt{neutralityScale})
$$

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hFcal`) — $0 \lt F_{\rm cal}$.
- **H2** (`hN`) — for every species $s$, $0 \lt N_s$.
- **H3** (`hI`) — for every species $s$, $I_s = L(k_B,T,N_s,F_{\rm cal},U_s,\ell_s)$.
- **H4** (`hu`) — for every species $s$, $J_s = L(k_B,T,1,1,U_s,\ell_s)$.
- **H5** (`hunit`) — for every species $s$, $0 \lt J_s$.

### 1.4 Conclusion

For any two species $a$ and $b$, the closure-normalized intensities satisfy

$$
\frac{C_a}{C_b}=\frac{N_a}{N_b}.
$$

Also, for every stage-ratio vector $R$ and electron density $n_e$ for which $Q_{R,n_e}\neq 0$, the neutrality-normalized per-unit intensities satisfy

$$
\frac{(I_a/J_a)/Q_{R,n_e}}{(I_b/J_b)/Q_{R,n_e}}=\frac{N_a}{N_b}.
$$

## 2. Physical meaning

The theorem says that, once each species is represented by one designated line and the `lineIntensityPerU` forward model is assumed, the per-unit intensity ratio $I_s/J_s=F_{\rm cal}N_s$ (the proof's internal `key` step, checked by Lean inside this proof but not exported as a declaration) contains no partition-function dependence: the partition-function input $U_s$ appears in both defining equations and cancels in the algebra. Consequently, both the closure normalization (sum over observed species) and the neutrality normalization (common scale $Q_{R,n_e}$) act as common-mode factors and cannot change a species ratio [derivation, unchecked].

The result is a normalization-invariance identity, not an accuracy statement. It does not model missing species, measurement noise, or absolute calibration. The formal quantification over arbitrary `R` and `ne` is compatible with readings in which `ne` includes charge from undetected species, but that reading is not part of the Lean statement [derivation, unchecked].

## 3. Scope and validity

The generated tags line will be inserted by `gen_cards.py`; it is not hand-typed here.

- **Idealizations encoded:** The statement is algebra over the `lineIntensityPerU` forward model: one designated line per species, a shared real temperature and Boltzmann constant, a shared calibration factor, and a free per-species partition-function input. It does not assume $k_B \gt 0$, $T \gt 0$, $U_s \gt 0$, $g_\ell \gt 0$, or $A_\ell \gt 0$; H1, H2 and H5 are the only positivity hypotheses, although H4 with H5 forces $A_{\ell_s}g_{\ell_s}/U_s\gt0$ for each designated line. The physical interpretation as an LTE, optically thin, single-temperature photon-rate model comes from the forward-model definition, not from additional hypotheses in this theorem [derivation, unchecked].
- **What it does NOT claim:** It does not claim that the absolute composition $N$ or calibration $F_{\rm cal}$ is accurate, identifiable, or unbiased. It does not claim that `neutralityScale` recovers a physically correct electron density; the second identity only assumes that scale is nonzero. It does not model undetected species as a source of bias, measurement noise, time-integrated temperature averaging, or multi-line mixing. It does not claim that ratio mode improves measurement accuracy. $N_a/N_b$ is a ratio of emitting-stage (neutral) densities. The theorem does not claim it equals the elemental ratio $(N_a/N_b)(1+R_a)/(1+R_b)$, which differs whenever the two species' ionized fractions differ [derivation, unchecked]; that stage-fraction channel is the subject of `ratioEstimate_hasDerivAt`'s reading. It does not claim that neutrality normalization improves ratio-mode estimates; by this identity it cannot. Its content is the absolute scale (`neutralityScale_eq_Fcal`), and its bias is the undetected species' charge share (`neutralityScale_undetected`).
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** A witness is $\kappa=\iota=\mathrm{Fin}\,2$, `emit` identity, $g_\ell=A_\ell=U_s=1$, $E_\ell=0$, $k_B=T=F_{\rm cal}=1$, $N=(1,2)$, $I_s=N_s$, and $J_s=1$. Then H1-H5 hold, the closure ratio is $1/2$, and $R_s=1$, $n_e=1$ gives $Q_{R,n_e}=3\neq0$ [derivation, unchecked]. If $F_{\rm cal}=0$, the closure estimates are zero in the Lean forward-model algebra, so H1 is doing real work [derivation, unchecked]. H1 and H2 are not sharp. The proof uses only $F_{\rm cal}\ne0$ and $\sum_t N_t\ne0$ for the closure identity, and only $F_{\rm cal}\ne0$ for the neutrality identity [derivation, unchecked]. H2 cannot simply be dropped: with $N=(1,-1)$, H1 and H3–H5 hold, both closure estimates are $0/0=0$ in Lean, and the left side is $0$ while $N_a/N_b=-1$. H5 carries real content through H4: $J_s\gt0$ forces $A_{\ell_s}g_{\ell_s}/U_s\gt0$.

## 4. Proof idea

The proof first derives the local cancellation identity $I_s/J_s=F_{\rm cal}N_s$ by rewriting `hI` and `hu`, using `hunit` to apply `div_eq_iff`, unfolding `lineIntensityPerU`, and simplifying with `ring`. It then proves $0 < \sum_t N_t$ from `hN` using `Finset.sum_pos` and the witness `a`.

For the closure conjunct, it unfolds `closureEstimate`, substitutes the cancellation identity, uses `Finset.mul_sum` to factor $F_{\rm cal}$ from the sum, and finishes with `field_simp` using H1 and the positive sum. For the neutrality conjunct, it rewrites with `div_div_div_cancel_right₀` under the local nonzero-scale hypothesis, substitutes the cancellation identity for both species, and finishes with `field_simp` using H1.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** The theorem is landed in `CflibsFormal.Alt.NeutralityScale`. Conjunct (i) (the closure-normalized ratio) is mirrored, unnamed, by CF-LIBS-improved `cflibs/domain/composition.py#log_ratios` (implicit reliance). No neutrality-normalized reader exists on CF-LIBS-improved main for conjunct (ii); `neutral` appears there only in a charge-neutrality consistency check in `cflibs/plasma/state.py`.
- **Proposed use:** It can serve as a machine-checked invariant for closure-free ratio mode: under the stated forward model, changing from closure normalization to neutrality normalization, or applying any common nonzero scale, cannot change a species ratio [proved: CflibsFormal.ratio_mode_normalization_invariant].
- **What it would change:** It would change rigor, not accuracy: it gives a formal guarantee that a normalization choice is ratio-invariant in the forward model, so pipeline consumers can rely on that invariant without re-deriving it. It does not pitch the theorem as improving measurement accuracy.
- **How it would be validated:** The proved identity cannot fail. The falsifiable claim is that a reader implements `closureEstimate` and `neutralityScale` as defined here. On a synthetic forward-model spectrum built from `lineIntensityPerU` with per-species $U_s$, the closure-normalized and neutrality-normalized ratios must agree with each other and with the injected $N_a/N_b$ to floating-point tolerance, for arbitrary $R$ and $n_e$ with $Q\ne0$. Any disagreement beyond rounding falsifies the implementation.
