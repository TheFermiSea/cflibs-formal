### 1.2 Definitions used

- `equivWidth φ τ := ∫ x, 1 - Real.exp (-(τ * φ x))` — a Bochner integral over all of $\mathbb{R}$
  against Lebesgue measure.
- `stepProfile η M := fun x => (Set.Icc 0 1).indicator (fun _ => 1) x + η * (Set.Icc 0 M).indicator (fun _ => 1) x`.
- `stepW η M τ := (1 - Real.exp (-(τ * (1 + η)))) + (M - 1) * (1 - Real.exp (-(τ * η)))`.
- `Set.InjOn`, `Set.Ioi`, `Set.Icc`, `Set.Ioc` — Mathlib set/order library.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

The headline theorem `stepProfile_pairRatio_not_injOn` has **no hypotheses**: it is an
unconditional negation, closed over the fixed numeric parameters $\eta = 1/100$, $M = 20$, $r = 2$.

### 1.4 Conclusion

Let $\psi = \psi_{1/100,\,20}$ and $R(n) = W_\psi(2n)\,/\,W_\psi(n)$. Then

$$
\neg\,\operatorname{InjOn}\bigl(R,\ (0,\infty)\bigr),
$$

i.e. there exist $n_1 \ne n_2$, both in $(0,\infty)$, with $R(n_1) = R(n_2)$.

## 2. Physical meaning

Read $\psi$ as a line profile over a frequency or wavelength offset $x$, in units of the core
width: a strong narrow core (height $1.01$ on $[0,1]$) sitting on a weak broad wing (height $0.01$
out to $x = 20$). $W$ is the equivalent width of a homogeneous self-absorbing slab, and $n$ the
lower-level column density of one species; two lines of that species sharing the profile, the
source and $n$ have opacity coefficients in ratio $r$, so their optical-depth scales are $rn$ and
$n$. The observable pair ratio $I_1/I_2 = W(rn)/W(n)$ has the unknown common source cancelled, so
injectivity of that ratio in $n$ is exactly what would let a measured ratio pin the column density.

The step profile breaks this at $r = 2$. `[numerics: evidence/ft20.step-profile-pair-ratio-counterexample/numerics.out]`
As $n \to 0^+$, $R \to 2$ (both lines optically thin). As the core saturates, $R$ falls to an
interior minimum $\approx 1.382$ near $n \approx 2.42$ — the same falling branch the flat kernel
follows. But the thin wing (total weight $19 \times 0.01 = 0.19$ beyond $x=1$) keeps growing
roughly linearly until it too saturates, near $n\eta \sim 1$, pushing $R$ back up to an interior
maximum $\approx 1.635$ near $n \approx 20.2$ before it finally falls toward $1$ as $n \to \infty$
`[derivation, unchecked]`.
A measured ratio of $1.45$ is therefore consistent with three column densities,
$n \approx 1.30$, $4.67$ and $69.9$
`[numerics: evidence/ft20.step-profile-pair-ratio-counterexample/numerics.out]`; the first two are
the numerical locations of the IVT points the proof obtains in $[1,3]$ and $[3,10]$.

This saturate-then-regrow mechanism is qualitatively what a Stark-broadened line does: a Doppler
core saturates while Lorentzian wings keep growing. The step profile is an elementary surrogate
that makes the effect provable without transcendental-function analysis. `[derivation, unchecked]`
For real Voigt profiles the claim is numerics only (FT-20(c), open item, not stated in Lean): the
audit's probes ($r=2$, line-centre depths $\le 30$) find the ratio non-monotone at the probed
$\gamma/\sigma = 0.1, 0.3, 0.93$ (no larger $\gamma/\sigma$ probed), rising only $0.6\%$, $1.6\%$
and $0.4\%$ past the minimum by depth $30$, and monotone at $\gamma/\sigma = 0.01$
(`CurveOfGrowth.lean` module scope block; audit numerics, not replayed here) — so for realistic
line shapes the practical concern is conditioning, not the gross two-valued ambiguity the Lean
proof exhibits for the surrogate profile. The Lean result claims none of the Voigt discussion.

## 3. Scope and validity

PLACEHOLDER: the generated tags-line sentence goes here once `gen_cards.py` fills it; leave a
placeholder paragraph until then. Under the D15 two-axis rule a `def` needs a scope row only when

- **Idealizations encoded.** A homogeneous slab (one optical-depth scale times one fixed profile),

- **What it does NOT claim.**
  - That any real spectral line has the two-step profile; it is a proof device, chosen because it
    closes in elementary form.
  - That a Voigt or Stark-broadened line is non-injective in the pair ratio — that is numerics
    only (FT-20(c), open item), not a Lean statement.
  - That `cogRatio_injOn` or the C13 certificate `saDistinct_certificate_sound` is wrong: both
    remain true and proved, for the flat kernel they are actually stated over.
  - Anything about opacity ratios $r \ne 2$, or about other $(\eta, M)$ parameter choices.
  - Anything about *relative composition*: $n$ is one species' column density, not a mixing ratio.
  - Anything about the behaviour of the CF-LIBS-improved pipeline's own code (§5 describes only
    what that code currently assumes, not anything this Lean result computes about it).
- **Hypothesis sharpness and non-vacuity.** The `hM : 1 ≤ M` hypothesis of `equivWidth_stepProfile`

  `[numerics: evidence/ft20.step-profile-pair-ratio-counterexample/numerics.out]`. The
  counterexample itself is non-vacuous by construction: the proof obtains (non-constructively, by

## 4. Proof idea

1. **Closed form (`equivWidth_stepProfile`).** Split the integrand pointwise into an indicator on
   $[0,1]$ plus one on $(1,M]$ (`Set.indicator_of_mem`/`_of_notMem`); `MeasureTheory.integral_add`,
   `integral_indicator_const` and `integrable_indicator_iff` (with `measure_Icc_lt_top.ne` /
   `measure_Ioc_lt_top.ne`) turn the two pieces' measures into the literal lengths $1$ and $M-1$
   via `Real.volume_Icc`/`Ioc` and `ENNReal.toReal_ofReal`.
2. **Two exponential enclosures.** `Real.add_one_le_exp` gives $e^{-1/100}\in[0.99,0.9901]$;
   `Real.exp_one_gt_d9`/`lt_d9` then give $e^{-101/100}\in[0.3642,0.3643]$; `Real.exp_nat_mul`
   rewrites every exponential at $n\in\{1,2,3,6,10,20\}$ as an integer power of these two bases.
3. **Three values by `nlinarith`.** `pow_le_pow_left₀` bounds each power; `nlinarith`/`div_lt_iff₀`/
   `lt_div_iff₀` close $R(1) > 29/20$, $R(3) < 29/20$, $R(10) > 29/20$ (private helpers
   `stepW_ratio1_gt`, `stepW_ratio3_lt`, `stepW_ratio10_gt`/`stepW_ratio10_uv`).
4. **IVT, twice.** `stepW_ratio_continuousOn` gives continuity of $n\mapsto S(2n)/S(n)$ on $[1,10]$
   (`ContinuousOn.div`, denominator positive via `Real.exp_lt_one_iff`); `intermediate_value_Icc'`
   on $[1,3]$ and `intermediate_value_Icc` on $[3,10]$ each give a point at ratio $29/20$;
   injectivity would force both to equal $3$, contradicting $R(3) < 29/20$.
5. **Bridge.** One `simpa only [equivWidth_stepProfile (by norm_num : (1:ℝ) ≤ 20), stepW]` rewrite
   turns `stepW_pairRatio_not_injOn` into `stepProfile_pairRatio_not_injOn`.

All helpers used in steps 2–4 are `private` to `EquivalentWidth.lean` (not separately carded).

`cogRatio_injOn`) states the flat-kernel/common-source restriction this card is the machine-checked
reason for. In the inversion-side runtime-certificate wiring (`certificates_wiring.py`,
default-off) C13 is a documented skip because curve-of-growth widths are not materialized on the
doublet-ratio path; the evolution-side `certificate_gate.py` evaluates C13 only when a witness

`correct_via_doublet_ratio` (`cflibs/inversion/physics/self_absorption.py`) and its fixed-step JIT
mirror `_bisect_tau` (`cflibs/jitpipe/selfabs.py`) — solves a flat-kernel escape-factor residual
with a bracketed root-finder and returns exactly one root, with no ambiguity flag. Within the flat
kernel that root is in fact unique (the Lean sibling `doubletRatio_injOn`, relation REDUCED,
proves injectivity of the same family at $\rho \ne 1$, via the identity
$f(\tau_1)/f(\tau_1/\rho) = \operatorname{doubletRatio}(\rho, \tau_1/\rho)/\rho$
`[derivation, unchecked]`); this card's contribution is that the uniqueness is a property of the
flat kernel the solvers assume, not of the line being measured. The companion already has a
non-flat escape factor elsewhere (`doppler_cog_escape_factor`,
`cflibs/inversion/physics/self_absorption_observable.py`, a different code path), though its
documented validity range ($\tau_0 \le 3$) is narrower than the pair solvers' bracket, so a
non-flat kernel is not a new capability to add, only one not yet connected to the pair/doublet
solve.

**Proposed use.**

1. **Documentation only, no behaviour change.** Correct the two stale docstrings above to say
   "opacity coefficients", drop the composition claim, and add the flat-kernel/common-source
   caveat, pointing at this card.
2. **A root-count guard, for a future non-flat pair solver only.** *Before* any pair or doublet
   solver stops assuming a flat kernel, scan its residual on a grid over the solver's optical-depth
   range and count sign changes; more than one means reporting every root and abstaining, not
   returning one silently. For the flat kernel this guard is provably redundant (at most one root,
   per `doubletRatio_injOn`); for a Doppler or Voigt kernel there is no Lean theorem, so a
   root-count guard is the only protection until one exists.

**What it would change.** Item 1 removes an overclaim from two docstrings; the numbers those
functions return are unchanged. Item 2 changes an output only for a solver that does not exist
today and only once it stops assuming a flat kernel — it turns a silent single answer into an
explicit ambiguity report in exactly the case this card exhibits. Neither item claims or targets
any improvement in measurement accuracy; the point is honest scope, not a better number.

**How it would be validated.** Use the step profile itself as a test fixture for item 2, with an
explicit falsification arm:
- At the target ratio $29/20$ the guard must report at least two roots. This value comes from
  inside the proof, not from the statement (which says only `¬InjOn`): the proof's IVT step places
  one point in $[1,3]$ and one in $[3,10]$, located numerically at $n \approx 1.297$ and $4.668$,
  both inside $[10^{-4}, 30]$; a third crossing at $n \approx 69.9$ lies outside that bracket
  `[numerics: evidence/ft20.step-profile-pair-ratio-counterexample/numerics.out]`.
- For the flat kernel the guard must never report more than one root (`cogRatio_injOn`: at most
  one); it must report exactly one for targets inside the bracket's image
  $\bigl(1+e^{-30},\ 1+e^{-10^{-4}}\bigr)$, by continuity and strict monotonicity of $1+e^{-n}$
  `[derivation, unchecked]`.
- **Falsification arm:** the same test with the guard disabled must fail — i.e. must demonstrate
  that a disabled guard silently returns one root at the step-profile target ratio, exactly the
  failure mode item 2 exists to catch. A guard that "passes" with or without being active is not a
  guard.
