### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hδ`) — positive margin: $0 \lt \delta$.
- **H2** (`hdom`) — row diagonal dominance with margin: for every $k\in L$,

\delta + \sum_{j\in L\setminus\{k\}} |(K^\top K)_{kj}| \le |(K^\top K)_{kk}|.

$$
(K^\top K)\hat I = K^\top(K_t I + \eta).
$$

For every line index $l\in L$,
$$
|\hat I_l - I_l| \le \frac{1}{\delta} \max_{k\in L} \left| \bigl(K^\top((K_t-K)I+\eta)\bigr)_k \right|.
$$

## 2. Physical meaning

The mathematical content is a linear-algebra error bound. Under a reduced physics reading, $P$ indexes detector pixels and $L$ spectral lines; $K_t I$ is the optically thin additive line signal and $\eta$ is additive noise [derivation, unchecked]. The extractor uses an assumed kernel $K$ and returns any vector $\hat I$ satisfying the normal equations. The theorem says that, if the assumed Gram matrix is row-diagonally dominant with margin $\delta$, then the per-line intensity error is bounded by the assumed misspecification-plus-noise residual divided by $\delta$.

## 3. Scope and validity

The generated tags line will be inserted here by tooling.

- **Idealizations encoded:** finite pixel and line sets, real-valued matrices, an additive linear forward model, exact satisfaction of the normal equations, a kernel fixed for the solver step, unweighted and unconstrained least-squares stationarity (no per-pixel weights, no nonnegativity or box constraints on $\hat I$), and no self-absorption or other nonlinear spectral effects [derivation, unchecked].
- **What it does NOT claim:**
  - It does not claim that the residual $K^\top((K_t-K)I+\eta)$ is small, known, or computable at runtime; $K_t$ and $\eta$ are unknown inputs to the bound [derivation, unchecked].
  - It does not claim that a given instrument kernel satisfies H1 or H2, or that the margin $\delta$ is large [derivation, unchecked].
  - It does not claim that the extraction is accurate, nor that using this bound improves measurement accuracy.
  - It does not handle self-absorption, nonlinear line mixing, or iteration-dependent kernels.
  - H1–H2 imply $K^\top K$ is nonsingular (mathlib `det_ne_zero_of_sum_row_lt_diag`), so $\hat I$ is in fact unique; the theorem does not use this.
  - It does not prove the closed-form bias identity $\hat I - I = (K^\top K)^{-1}K^\top((K_t-K)I+\eta)$ (audit item, unlanded); only the normal-equation error identity is proved, as the private helper `normal_error_identity`.
  - Strongly blended lines have overlapping profile columns and large off-diagonal Gram entries, so H2 typically fails for them, and the theorem then gives nothing [derivation, unchecked].
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** Take $P=L$ a singleton, $K=1$, $K_t=2$, $I=1$, $\hat I=2$, $\eta=0$, $\delta=1$. H1–H3 hold, and $|\hat I-I| = 1$ equals the right-hand side, so the bound is attained and the factor $1/\delta$ cannot be improved in general [scratch: evidence/witness.lean]. H1 is needed: with $\delta=0$ Lean's $a/0=0$ makes the right-hand side $0$.

## 4. Proof idea

The proof first derives the exact error identity
$$
(K^\top K)(\hat I-I)=K^\top((K_t-K)I+\eta)
$$
from H3 by linearity of matrix-vector multiplication. In Lean this is the private helper `normal_error_identity`, proved with `Matrix.mulVec_sub`, `Matrix.sub_mulVec`, `Matrix.mulVec_mulVec`, `Matrix.mulVec_add`, and `abel`.

The second step applies the row-diagonal-dominance ℓ∞ lemma `linfty_le_of_rowDiagDominant` to $M=K^\top K$ and $x=\hat I-I$. That lemma uses `Finset.exists_max_image`, `Finset.le_sup'`, `Finset.add_sum_erase`, `Finset.abs_sum_le_sum_abs`, `abs_sub`, `abs_mul`, `le_div_iff₀`, and `nlinarith`. Finally, the headline theorem rewrites with `normal_error_identity` and `Pi.sub_apply`.

- **Proposed use:** It can serve as an a-priori error bound for line-intensity extraction when the assumed kernel is misspecified. The margin $\delta$ is the part that can be checked from the assumed kernel alone, while the right-hand side is an assumed-input bound on misspecification plus noise [derivation, unchecked]. Downstream, it could be combined with an intensity-to-ordinate corollary to feed an error budget.
- **What it would change:** It would change rigor, not accuracy: the dependence of extracted line intensities on profile misspecification and noise would be explicit, bounded, and partly checkable through the margin [derivation, unchecked].
- **How it would be validated:** The Lean statement is proved; the falsifiable claim is its application. On synthetic spectra with known $K_t$, $\eta$, check H1–H3 for the extractor's own $K$ and output; an observed error above the right-hand side means the extractor does not satisfy H3 (weights, constraints, non-convergence) or the encoding differs.
