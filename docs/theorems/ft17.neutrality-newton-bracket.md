## 1. Statement

At fixed temperature $T$, write $\iota$ for the finite index type of chemical species
(`[Fintype ι]`). Each species `s : ι` carries a Saha factor $S_s$ and a total (neutral + singly
ionized) elemental density $\mathrm{Ntot}_s$. Write
$$
G(x) := \sum_{s} \frac{\mathrm{Ntot}_s \cdot S_s}{x + S_s}, \qquad
D(x) := \sum_{s} \frac{\mathrm{Ntot}_s \cdot S_s}{(x + S_s)^2}, \qquad
N(x) := x - \frac{x - G(x)}{1 + D(x)}
$$
for an electron-density value $x \in \mathbb{R}$; both sums range over `Finset.univ : Finset ι`.
$G$ is the multi-element ionized-density closure map (`CflibsFormal.multiElementIonized S Ntot`);
charge neutrality is its fixed point $x = G(x)$. $N$ is Newton's method on the residual
$f(x) := x - G(x)$, whose derivative is $f'(x) = 1 + D(x)$: `CflibsFormal.neutralityNewton S Ntot`.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

Shared across the headline and members that use them (each member's own subsection says which
apply):

- **H1** (`hS`) — $\forall s,\ S_s > 0$: every Saha factor is positive, so every denominator
  $x + S_s$, $r + S_s$ below is provably positive whenever $x, r \ge 0$.
- **H2** (`hN`) — $\forall s,\ \mathrm{Ntot}_s \ge 0$: every elemental density is nonnegative (a species
  may be entirely absent, $\mathrm{Ntot}_s = 0$); this is what makes $G$ antitone and $f' \ge 1$.
- **H3** (`hx`) — $x \ge 0$: the guess lies on the physical half-line.
- **H4** (`hr`) — $r \ge 0$: the selected root is nonnegative. This rules out a second,
  *unphysical* negative root that the bare equation $r = G(r)$ does not exclude by itself — see
  §3's sharpness witness.
- **H5** (`hfix`) — $r = G(r)$: charge neutrality holds at $r$.

### 1.4 Conclusion (headline: `neutralityNewton_enclosure`)

Under H1–H5, one Newton step from any guess brackets the root on both sides:
$$
N(x) \le r \qquad\text{and}\qquad r \le G(N(x)).
$$
Both ends of the interval $[N(x), G(N(x))]$ are computable from $x$ alone — neither end needs $r$.
No `Nonempty ι` hypothesis is required: with no species, or with every $\mathrm{Ntot}_s = 0$, $G \equiv 0$,
so $r = 0$ and $N(x) = 0$ for every $x$, and the bracket $[0, 0]$ holds trivially.

### 1.7 Member: `neutralityNewton_error_eq`

**Hypotheses:** H1 (`hS`), H2 (`hN`), H3 (`hx`), H4 (`hr`), H5 (`hfix`).

**Conclusion:** an *exact* algebraic identity for the Newton step's error, not an inequality:
$$
N(x) - r = \frac{-(x - r)^2 \cdot \displaystyle\sum_{s} \frac{\mathrm{Ntot}_s \cdot S_s}{(x+S_s)^2(r+S_s)}}
                 {1 + D(x)}.
$$
The numerator's sum is a finite sum of nonnegative terms (H1–H4), so the whole numerator is
$\le 0$; dividing by the positive $1 + D(x)$ (H1, H2) gives $N(x) - r \le 0$ directly — this is the
one-line route to `neutralityNewton_le_root` (§1.8) with no separate convexity argument. Lean
correspondence: same as §1.5; the leading minus sign is literal in the Lean statement's `-(...)`,
not a typesetting choice.

### 1.8 Member: `neutralityNewton_le_root`

**Hypotheses:** H1, H2, H3, H4, H5 (identical to §1.7).

**Conclusion:** $N(x) \le r$ — the Newton step from any guess never *overshoots* the root from
above. Lean correspondence: same as §1.5.

### 1.9 Member: `neutralityNewton_nonneg`

**Hypotheses:** H1 (`hS`), H2 (`hN`), H3 (`hx`) only — **no** `r`, `hr` or `hfix`; this fact needs

## 2. Physical meaning

The multi-element charge-neutrality closure models one two-stage Saha balance per chemical
species, all coupled through a single shared electron density $x$: species $s$ contributes
ionized density $\mathrm{Ntot}_s \cdot S_s/(x+S_s)$ to the plasma, and charge neutrality is the point where
the sum of these contributions equals $x$ itself, $x = G(x)$. $N$ is literally Newton's method for
finding that point: it takes the residual $f(x) = x - G(x)$ and its derivative $f'(x) = 1 + D(x)$
and produces the usual Newton update $x - f(x)/f'(x)$. What is physically new here is not the
Newton step (a generic numerical-analysis construction) but what it buys for *this particular* $G$:
because $G$ is antitone (at the fixed $T$, a larger electron-density guess $x$ lowers every
species' ionized fraction $S_s/(x+S_s)$, so the ionized-density sum falls), a single Newton step
below the root, fed back through $G$, lands above the root — so one evaluation of $G$ at $N(x)$
turns a one-sided estimate into a two-sided, certified enclosure of the closure's nonnegative root
$r$ (the model's charge-neutral electron density at the supplied $S$, $Ntot$, not a measured
plasma $n_e$), without ever having solved for it.

## 4. Proof idea

The whole family rests on one algebraic fact already proved elsewhere in the module,
`multiElementIonized_two_point` (the exact two-point/discrete-slope identity
$G(x) - G(y) = (y-x)\sum_s \mathrm{Ntot}_s S_s/((x+S_s)(y+S_s))$ for $x, y \ge 0$): substituting $y = r$ and
using H5 turns $x - G(x)$ into $(x-r)\cdot(1 + \sum_s \dots)$, and a further finite-sum
rearrangement (`Finset.sum_sub_distrib`, `Finset.mul_sum`, `field_simp`, `ring`) isolates the
$-(x-r)^2(\dots)$ numerator of `neutralityNewton_error_eq` — this is `nlinarith`/`linarith`-closed
once the rearrangement is in place, no case split. `neutralityNewton_le_root` then reads the sign
off that identity directly (`Finset.sum_nonneg`, `div_nonpos_of_nonpos_of_nonneg`).
`neutralityNewton_nonneg` rewrites $N(x)$ into the equivalent form
$(x\cdot D(x) + G(x))/(1+D(x))$ and applies `div_nonneg` to a sum of nonnegative terms, using a
private helper `multiElementIonized_nonneg_of` for $G(x) \ge 0$. `neutralityNewton_enclosure`
composes `neutralityNewton_le_root` (giving $N(x) \le r$) and `neutralityNewton_nonneg` (giving
$N(x) \ge 0$) with the private antitonicity helper `multiElementIonized_antitone_of` (built from the same
two-point identity, this time with the sign of `Finset.sum_nonneg` read the other way) then gives
$G(N(x)) \ge G(r) = r$ from $0 \le N(x) \le r$ — the last step rewriting through H5 (`rw [← hfix]`).
No convexity lemma, no derivative machinery and no case analysis on `Nonempty ι` is used anywhere.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state.** No shipped pipeline code imports this Lean development — Python and Lean are
  not linked at runtime anywhere in this repo pair — so "role" here means conceptual
  correspondence, not a runtime dependency. The closure map $G$ these theorems are about
  (`multiElementIonized`) is the same *structure* as two pipeline computations, at two different
  levels of fidelity: (1) `cflibs/plasma/anderson_solver.py`'s `picard_solve`/`anderson_solve` run
  Picard (m=0) / Anderson-accelerated iteration on a related forward charge-balance fixed point, in
  log $n_e$ at fixed $T$ and known heavy-particle density, but with Saha factors that carry an
  $n_e$-dependent Debye–Hückel lowering and sum every available stage — so they match
  `multiElementIonized` only on an IPD-off, two-stage configuration — and are plain substitution,
  not a Newton step; the module's own docstring says neither is imported by any shipped `cflibs`
  module (research/benchmark use only). (2) `cflibs/inversion/solve/iterative.py`'s
  `IterativeCFLIBSSolver._pressure_balance_ne` — the shipped outer loop's last-resort $n_e$
  fallback, used only when no SB-offset, Stark or Balmer $n_e$ is available — evaluates an isobaric
  1-atm balance from a three-stage (I/II/III) per-species Saha ladder with the heavy-particle total
  itself a function of $n_e$, so even on its $S_2 = 0$ slice it is not `multiElementIonized` at a
  fixed $\mathrm{Ntot}$. The outer loop (`IterativeCFLIBSSolver._run_python_iteration`) folds
  whichever $n_e$ estimate a step produces (by default the SB inter-stage offset, else Stark, else
  Balmer, else this pressure-balance fallback) in with a fixed, uncertified 0.5/0.5 Picard damping
  — not a Newton step on `multiElementIonized`, and not solving its fixed-$(S, \mathrm{Ntot})$
  closure at all; listed in `pipeline[]` only to rule it out (`relation: none`). Certificate C10 in
  `registry/certificates.yaml` backs the forward fixed-$T$ closure at known $\mathrm{Ntot}$ with a
  damped-iteration convergence theorem (`CflibsFormal.dampedMultiElementIter_tendsto`; Python
  twins `picard_solve`/`anderson_solve`); its own note says it does not replace the inverse loop's
  0.5 damping. This family is a complementary fact about the *undamped* single Newton step on the
  same forward closure, and is not wired to any certificate.
- **Proposed use.** Restricted to an IPD-off, two-stage configuration of `anderson_solver.py`'s
  forward fixed-$T$ closure (where its Picard/Anderson iteration and `multiElementIonized` agree),
  the bracket $[N(x), G(N(x))]$ is computable from a single extra evaluation of $G$ at whatever
  $n_e$ that solver's current iterate is, with no knowledge of the true root. That makes it a
  candidate per-call *certificate* on `picard_solve`/`anderson_solve`, not a replacement solver:
  after any step, compute $N$ of the current iterate and confirm the bracket's width
  $G(N(x)) - N(x)$ is shrinking, as a machine-checkable stopping/sanity signal layered on top of
  the existing iteration — restricted, honestly, to configurations where that solver's closure
  reduces to the two-stage, IPD-off case this family actually covers (§3).
- **What it would change.** Rigor, not accuracy (per `AGENTS.md`): wiring this bracket would add a
  proof-backed *stopping/consistency check* to `picard_solve`/`anderson_solve`'s bookkeeping — a
  machine-checked interval guaranteed to contain the closure's nonnegative root $r$ at the
  supplied $(S, \mathrm{Ntot})$ (not the current iterate, and not a measured plasma $n_e$) — not a
  change to the numerical accuracy of any recovered composition. The two-stage closure
  `multiElementIonized` models inherits that idealization exactly wherever it is applied, and
  would not correct or extend it.
- **How it would be validated.** A Python twin of $G$ and $N$ (the exact §1 formulas), with the
  reference root $r$ found by bisection on the twin (as in `witness.py` part A), checked across
  random $S > 0$, $\mathrm{Ntot} \ge 0$, $x \ge 0$. **Falsification arm:** any instance with an
  independently bisected root $r \ge 0$ where the twin reports $N(x) > r + \text{tol}$ or
  $G(N(x)) < r - \text{tol}$ falsifies the twin, or this card's reading of the definitions.
  Separately, on an `anderson_solver` configuration with IPD on (so each $S_s$ depends on $n_e$),
  exhibit an instance where the bracket computed with frozen $S$ fails to contain the IPD-aware
  root. That confirms the fixed-$S$ restriction is load-bearing, and not merely cosmetic.
