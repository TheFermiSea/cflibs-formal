## 1. Statement

The family concerns the finite Boltzmann sum over a supplied level list and its Boltzmann-weighted
mean excitation energy. All quantities are real numbers; the level index type is finite and
nonempty by instance arguments.

### 1.1 Symbols

| Symbol | Lean name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $\iota$ | `ι` | finite nonempty index type for the supplied level list |
| $k_B$ | `kB` | Boltzmann constant, a real number |
| $T_1,T_2$ | `T1`,`T2` | two temperatures in the headline bound |
| $T$ | `T` | a generic temperature, used by the members |
| $g_k$ | `g` | level weight or degeneracy, a function $\iota\to\mathbb R$ |
| $E_k$ | `E` | excitation energy of level $k$, a function $\iota\to\mathbb R$ |
| $U(T)$ | `partitionFunction kB T g E` | Boltzmann partition sum over the supplied levels |
| $\langle E\rangle_T$ | `meanExcitation kB T g E` | Boltzmann-weighted mean excitation energy |
| $\beta(T)$ | `1 / (kB * T)` | inverse temperature, written as a Lean expression |
| $M(w,b,a)$ | `mixture w b a` | discrete Laplace mixture used by the bridge lemmas |
| $\bar b(w,b,a)$ | `tiltMean w b a` | tilted mean of the abscissae $b$ |

### 1.2 Definitions used

- `partitionFunction` — the finite Boltzmann sum
  $$
  U(T)=\sum_{k\in\iota} g_k \exp\!\left(-\frac{E_k}{k_B T}\right).
  $$
- `meanExcitation` — the Boltzmann-weighted mean level energy
  $$
  \langle E\rangle_T
  =
  \frac{\sum_{k\in\iota} g_k E_k \exp\!\left(-E_k/(k_B T)\right)}{U(T)}.
  $$
  Lean division is totalized: the value is $0$ if $U(T)=0$. Under positive weights on a nonempty
  level set, $U(T)>0$, so this totalization is not exercised in the hypotheses of the headline
  theorem.
- `mixture` — the discrete Laplace transform
  $$
  M(w,b,a)=\sum_z w_z \exp(-a b_z).
  $$
- `tiltMean` — the tilted mean of the abscissae
  $$
  \bar b(w,b,a)
  =
  \sum_z \frac{w_z \exp(-a b_z)}{M(w,b,a)}\,b_z.
  $$

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hkB`) — $0 \lt k_B$.
- **H2** (`hT1`) — $0 \lt T_1$.
- **H3** (`hT2`) — $0 \lt T_2$.
- **H4** (`hg`) — for every level $k$, $0 \lt g_k$.
- **H5** (`hE`) — for every level $k$, $0 \le E_k$.

### 1.4 Conclusion

Under H1–H5, the log partition function satisfies the two-point inverse-temperature bound

$$
| \log U(T_1) - \log U(T_2) |
\le
\langle E\rangle_{\max(T_1,T_2)}
\,
\left| \frac{1}{k_B T_1} - \frac{1}{k_B T_2} \right|.
$$

Equivalently, with $\beta(T)=1/(k_B T)$,

$$
| \log U(T_1) - \log U(T_2) |
\le
\langle E\rangle_{\max(T_1,T_2)}
\,
| \beta(T_1) - \beta(T_2) |.
$$

## 2. Physical meaning

Read as a statement about one ionization stage in local thermodynamic equilibrium, $g_k$ and
$E_k$ are level degeneracies and excitation energies measured from the stage ground level, and
$U(T)$ is the partition sum over the supplied finite level list [derivation, unchecked]. The
LTE level fraction of level $k$ is

$$
p_k(T)=\frac{g_k \exp(-E_k/(k_B T))}{U(T)},
$$

so $\langle E\rangle_T=\sum_k p_k(T)E_k$ is the mean excitation energy under those level
fractions [derivation, unchecked].

The headline theorem says that the change in $\log U$ between two positive temperatures is
controlled by the mean excitation energy at the hotter temperature times the change in inverse
temperature. It is a two-point sensitivity bound for the log partition function in the
inverse-temperature variable $\beta=1/(k_B T)$ [derivation, unchecked].

The monotonicity member says that, for positive weights, the mean excitation energy cannot
decrease when temperature increases [proved: CflibsFormal.meanExcitation_monotoneOn_temp]. No
nonnegativity hypothesis on the energies is needed for that monotonicity
[proved: CflibsFormal.meanExcitation_monotoneOn_temp]. The bridge lemmas identify the Boltzmann
objects with the `mixture` and `tiltMean`
formalism, with level energies playing the role of abscissae and the inverse temperature playing
the role of the anchor [proved: CflibsFormal.partitionFunction_eq_mixture] [proved:
CflibsFormal.meanExcitation_eq_tiltMean].

## 4. Proof idea

The headline proof first reduces, by symmetry, to the case $T_a\le T_b$. It uses:

- `partitionFunction_pos` to show $U(T)>0$ under positive weights and nonempty index type.
- `partitionFunction_mono_temp` to show $U(T_a)\le U(T_b)$ when $T_a\le T_b$ and the energies are
  nonnegative; this gives the sign of $\log U(T_a)-\log U(T_b)$, then `Real.log_le_log` (with
  `partitionFunction_pos`) turns this into $\log U(T_a)\le\log U(T_b)$.
- `one_div_le_one_div_of_le`, `mul_pos` and `mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_left` to order the inverse
  temperatures $\beta(T_b)\le \beta(T_a)$.
- `logMixture_tangent_le` as the convexity/tangent crux for $\beta\mapsto \log M(g,E,\beta)$.
- `partitionFunction_eq_mixture` and `meanExcitation_eq_tiltMean` to rewrite the mixture and
  tiltMean statement back into the Boltzmann partition-function and mean-excitation notation.
- `abs_of_nonpos`, `abs_of_nonneg`, `abs_sub_comm`, `max_eq_left`, `max_eq_right`, `le_total`,
  and `linarith` to remove the absolute values and handle the two orderings of $T_1,T_2$.

The monotonicity member is proved through `meanExcitation_eq_tiltMean` and the pair-slope
sandwich lemmas `tiltMean_le_pairSlope` and `pairSlope_le_tiltMean`, with the inverse-temperature
ordering supplied by `one_div_lt_one_div_of_lt` and `mul_lt_mul_of_pos_left`. The equal-temperature
case is trivial.
