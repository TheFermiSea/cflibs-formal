### 1.2 Definitions used

For fixed $\mathrm{grp}$ and $w$, and any $f,g : \iota\to\mathbb{R}$:

$$
\bar f_e = \frac{\sum_{k : \,\mathrm{grp}(k)=e} w_k f_k}{\sum_{k : \,\mathrm{grp}(k)=e} w_k}
\qquad(\texttt{gMean})
$$

$$
\langle f,g\rangle_W = \sum_k w_k\,(f_k-\bar f_{\mathrm{grp}(k)})(g_k-\bar g_{\mathrm{grp}(k)})
\qquad(\texttt{withinCross})
$$

$$
\mathrm{SS_W}(x) = \langle x,x\rangle_W
\qquad(\texttt{withinSS})
$$

$$
\hat\beta(y) = \frac{\langle x,y\rangle_W}{\mathrm{SS_W}(x)}
\qquad(\texttt{feSlope})
$$

with $\hat\beta(y)=0$ when $\mathrm{SS_W}(x)=0$ (Lean's $a/0=0$); only `feSlope_add_smul` can reach
that case.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hw`) — $\forall k,\ 0 < w_k$.

### 1.4 Conclusion

For every grouping $\mathrm{grp}$, weights $w$ and abscissa $x$ satisfying H1, the common slope is
identifiable exactly when the weighted within-group sum of squares of the abscissa is positive:

$$
\Bigl(\forall a,a':\kappa\to\mathbb{R},\ \forall \beta,\beta'\in\mathbb{R},\
(\forall k,\ a_{\mathrm{grp}(k)}+\beta\,x_k = a'_{\mathrm{grp}(k)}+\beta'\,x_k) \Rightarrow \beta=\beta'\Bigr)
\iff 0 < \mathrm{SS_W}(x).
$$

## 2. Physical meaning

The Lean statements are regression facts about a fixed-effects design: each line has a noiseless
ordinate of the form `a (grp k) + β * x k`, with one free intercept per group and one common slope.
In the physics reading that is not part of the Lean statement [derivation, unchecked], `ι` indexes
spectral lines, `κ` indexes elements, `grp` assigns each line to its element, and `x k` is the
IP-shifted upper-level energy. Under LTE with one temperature and IPD off or frozen, the common
slope is then `-1/(k_B T)` [derivation, unchecked].

The headline theorem says that a common slope is identifiable from noiseless ordinates exactly
when `withinSS > 0`, that is (under positive weights) when at least one element's kept lines do
not all share one abscissa [derivation, unchecked]. If every element group has constant abscissa,
the per-element intercepts can absorb a slope change, so the common slope is not identifiable.

`feSlope_add_smul` says that adding `c * s` to the ordinates shifts the fitted common slope by
`c` times the slope fitted to `s`. In the pipeline reading [derivation, unchecked], `s` can be an
ion-stage indicator and `c` a change in the `ln n_e` offset; this is the algebraic step behind an
offset-affinity relation for the fitted slope.

`feSlope_isMin` says that the within-centred slope, together with its implied per-element
intercepts, is a weighted least-squares minimizer for the fixed-effects model. It certifies the
solver's formula as a WLS fit, not merely as a plausible ratio [derivation, unchecked].

## 3. Scope and validity

- **Idealizations encoded:** The Lean statements assume a finite line set, decidable group equality,

- **What it does NOT claim:**
  - It does not claim that the fixed-effects minimizer is unique; `feSlope_isMin` proves only a
    minimizer inequality. Uniqueness is the separate `feSlope_unique_min` (through the residual
    split `feSlope_rss_split`), landed after this card and not a member of it.
  - It does not claim joint identifiability of a second covariate slope: the within Gram-determinant
    result (FT-04 part (iv)) is not landed in `CflibsFormal`.
  - It does not claim that the current pooled certificates C1-C3 are sound; the witness below shows
    that a pooled spread can be positive while `withinSS = 0`.
  - It does not claim a variance formula, bias bound, or any improvement in measurement accuracy.
  - It does not make the physics reading (LTE, one temperature, IPD off/frozen) part of the theorem.
  - It does not claim that `feSlope_add_smul` gives an identifiable slope when `withinSS = 0`;
    linearity holds, but identifiability can fail.
  - It gives no quantitative margin: any `withinSS > 0`, however small, counts as identifiable, and
    nothing bounds the fitted slope's sensitivity to noise.
  - It does not prove the variance reading `Var β̂ = σ²/SS_W`, and it defines no grouped certificate
    in `Certificates.lean`.
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** A concrete witness family is `ι = Fin 4`,
  `κ = Fin 2`, `grp = (0,0,1,1)`, and `w = 1`. For `x = (3,3,5,5)`, `withinSS = 0`; the parameter
  pair `a(e) = -gMean grp w x e`, `β = 1` and `a' = 0`, `β' = 0` gives the same noiseless ordinates,
  so the slope is not identifiable. For `x = (1,2,3,4)`, `withinSS = 1 > 0`, so the slope is
  identifiable. These witnesses show that both sides of the headline equivalence are non-vacuous.
  The same `x = (1,2,3,4)` witness satisfies `hw` and `hSS` for `feSlope_isMin`.

## 4. Proof idea

For `fe_identifiable_iff`, the repo helpers `fiber_dev_sum_zero` and `sum_dev_mul_groupConst`, which
say that weighted within-group deviations sum to zero in each group and are orthogonal to every
group-constant function, are used in the `(⇐)` direction and in `feSlope_isMin` below, not in
`(⇒)`. The `(⇒)` direction is by contraposition:
if `withinSS` is not positive, nonnegativity under `hw` gives `withinSS = 0`; termwise
nonnegativity then forces `x k = gMean grp w x (grp k)` for all `k`. The intercept witness
`a(e) = -gMean grp w x e`, `β = 1`, `a' = 0`, `β' = 0` contradicts identifiability using
`one_ne_zero`. The `(⇐)` direction writes the equality of ordinates as
`(β - β') * x k = a' (grp k) - a (grp k)`, applies `sum_dev_mul_groupConst` to the group-constant
intercept difference and to the group mean, obtains
`(β - β') * withinSS = 0`, and cancels using `hSS`. Mathlib lemmas include `Finset.sum_nonneg`,
`Finset.sum_eq_zero_iff_of_nonneg`, `Finset.mul_sum`, `Finset.sum_sub_distrib`,
`Finset.sum_congr`, `mul_self_eq_zero`, `linarith`, and `ring`.

`feSlope_add_smul` unfolds `feSlope` and uses `withinCross_add_smul`, `add_div`, and
`mul_div_assoc`. The helper `withinCross_add_smul` uses linearity of `gMean` in the averaged
function and distributivity of finite sums: `Finset.mul_sum`, `Finset.sum_add_distrib`,
`Finset.sum_congr`, and `ring`.

`feSlope_isMin` sets `bh := feSlope grp w x y`, uses `div_mul_cancel₀` under `hSS` to get
`bh * withinSS = withinCross`, rewrites both residual sums into within-centred form, and applies
`sum_dev_mul_groupConst` to kill the cross terms against group-constant differences. The private
helper `within_quad` expands the weighted quadratic in the slope. The final step combines
`Finset.sum_add_distrib`, `Finset.mul_sum`, `Finset.sum_sub_distrib`, `mul_nonneg`, `sq_nonneg`,
and `nlinarith`.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** The companion solver `_fit_common_boltzmann_plane` in
  `cflibs/inversion/solve/iterative.py` computes a within-centred common slope with per-element
  weighted centring and returns no fit when the denominator is not positive. The wired certificates
  C1 and C3 test the pooled upper-level energy spread, and C2 tests the pooled single-intercept
  joint design with an ion-stage indicator. The 2026-09-24 audit executed false positives in which
  a pooled spread was positive while `withinSS = 0`.
- **Proposed use:** Use `fe_identifiable_iff` to define a grouped design-rank certificate that
  tests `0 < withinSS grp w x` on the solver's kept lines. Use `feSlope_isMin` to justify the
  solver's within-centred formula as a weighted least-squares fixed-effects estimator. Use
  `feSlope_add_smul` for the FT-01 offset-affinity step: a change in the `ln n_e` offset shifts the
  fitted slope by `c` times the slope fitted to the ion-stage indicator. A grouped joint certificate
  would additionally need the joint Gram-determinant result, which is not landed.
- **What it would change:** It would change the rigor of design-rank checks: certificates would test
  the rank of the fixed-effects design rather than pooled spread. This is a rigor improvement, not
  an accuracy improvement; it does not claim to improve measurement accuracy.
- **How it would be validated:** The grouped certificate can be tested on explicit designs. A
  falsification arm is: if a grouped certificate passes on a design with `withinSS = 0`, or if a
  design with `withinSS > 0` admits two parameter sets with different slopes giving the same
  noiseless ordinates, then the certificate implementation or its use of the theorem is wrong.
  Concretely, `x = (3,3,5,5)` with `grp = (0,0,1,1)` and `w = 1` has positive pooled spread but
  `withinSS = 0`, so the grouped certificate should refuse; `x = (1,2,3,4)` with the same groups has
  `withinSS = 1`, so the grouped certificate should pass.
