$$
\frac{\tau_1}{\tau_2}

Equivalently, the common factors $N$, $\ell$, and $U(T)$ cancel from the optical-depth ratio, and
the per-line stimulated-emission factors $(1-e^{-x_i})$ remain [derivation, unchecked].

## 2. Physical meaning

In the LTE slab reading, the lower-level population is
$n_l=N g_l e^{-E_l/(k_B T)}/U(T)$, and the optical depth for a flat line-centre cross-section is
$\tau=\sigma_0\ell n_l$. For two lines of the same species, the density, path length, and partition
function cancel from the ratio, leaving a ratio of line-centre cross-sections, lower-level
degeneracies, and Boltzmann factors.

The factor $(1-e^{-x_i})$ is the stimulated-emission or negative-absorption factor in the LTE
absorption reading [cited: Aragón & Aguilera 2014, Eq. (4)]. This theorem carries that factor, as
part of each line's own $\sigma_{0i}$, through a per-line ratio identity, where a shared-$\sigma_0$
two-line bridge would hide it [derivation, unchecked].

## 3. Scope and validity

reading.

- **Idealizations encoded:** [derivation, unchecked] homogeneous single-temperature LTE slab; flat
  line-centre cross-section; finite level set; bare-real dimensionless quantities; $\kappa_{0i}$
  abstract; no line profile, spatial variation, instrument response, or atomic-constant model for
  $\kappa_{0i}$.
- **What it does NOT claim:** [derivation, unchecked] it does not claim a model-level identity for
  $\kappa_{0i}$ in terms of $\lambda_i$, $g_{u_i}$, $A_i$, or $\phi_i(0)$; it does not claim
  numerical sizes for $(1-e^{-x_i})$; it does not claim source-function Planck equality,
  emergent-intensity monotonicity, equivalent-width inequalities, or transfer-before-instrument
  results; it does not claim ordinary real division is defined when a denominator factor is zero;
  it does not claim measurement accuracy or calibration improvement. It does not tie $x_i$ to $T$,
  $k_B$ or the level energies: $x_1,x_2,\kappa_{01},\kappa_{02}$ are free reals, so the identity is
  the generic cancellation for two distinct cross-sections $\sigma_{0i}$ written in
  stimulated-emission form. It says nothing about the size, sign or temperature dependence of
  $(1-e^{-x_i})$; `opticalDepth` itself does not model stimulated emission, since the factor enters
  only through the $\sigma_0$ argument. It does not require $N$, $\ell$, $\kappa_{0i}$ or $\tau_i$
  to be positive.
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** H1–H3 are load-bearing: dropping any of them lets the
  left side be $0/0=0$ in Lean while the right side is nonzero (Lean docstring; the $N=0$ case
  re-checked in scratch [scratch: evidence/ft14iv.perline-tauratio-bridge/hN_needed.lean]). Only
  H3, with $l_1:\iota$ supplying nonemptiness, makes $U(T)$ positive via `partitionFunction_pos`.
  H4 alone is a domain guard: at $\sigma_{02}=0$ both sides are $0$ in Lean. A two-line witness is
  $\iota=\mathrm{Fin}\,2$, $l_1=0$, $l_2=1$, $E_0=0$, $E_1=1$, $\kappa_{01}=2$, $\kappa_{02}=3$,
  $x_1=0.5$, $x_2=1.5$, $k_B=T=N=\ell=1$, and $g_k=1$ for both levels [derivation, unchecked].

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** Not referenced by the pipeline. The pipeline's own per-line ratio
  `_tau_ratio` omits the per-line stimulated-emission factors and the lower-level Boltzmann ratio
  that this identity keeps (tension).
- **Proposed use:** Use as a guarded algebraic bridge when comparing two line optical depths in a
  self-absorption forward model or acceptance test. It can supply the per-line stimulated-emission
  factor in ratio identities where a shared-$\sigma_0$ bridge would hide it [derivation, unchecked].
- **What it would change:** [derivation, unchecked] It would change rigor, not accuracy: the
  cancellation of $N$, $\ell$, and $U(T)$, and the survival of $(1-e^{-x_i})$, become an exact lemma
  for the reduced slab model. It is not a claim that measurement accuracy improves.
- **How it would be validated:** [derivation, unchecked] Falsifiable by a two-line instance in the
  same slab model where a pipeline-computed $\tau$ ratio differs from the right-hand side. The
  falsification arm is a concrete pair of lines with nonzero parameters and distinct
  stimulated-emission factors for which the equality fails.
