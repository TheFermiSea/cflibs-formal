| $S_w$ | `wSS w E` | weighted centred sum of squares of the energies |
| $\lambda_k$ | `wlsWeight w E k` | weighted-least-squares slope weight |

### 1.2 Definitions used

$$
\bar E_w := \frac{\sum_k w_k E_k}{\sum_k w_k}
\qquad(\texttt{wMean})
$$

$$
S_w := \sum_k w_k (E_k - \bar E_w)^2
\qquad(\texttt{wSS})
$$

$$
\lambda_k := \frac{w_k (E_k - \bar E_w)}{S_w}
\qquad(\texttt{wlsWeight})
$$

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hw` binder) — for every line $k$, $0 \lt w_k$.
- **H2** (`hSS` binder) — $0 \lt S_w$: the slope is identifiable in the weighted energy spread.
- **H3** (`ha0` binder) — $\sum_k a_k = 0$ (the intercept drops out).
- **H4** (`ha1` binder) — $\sum_k a_k E_k = 1$ (together with H3: $\sum_k a_k(\alpha+\beta E_k)=\beta$
  for all $\alpha,\beta$).

### 1.4 Conclusion

With $\lambda_k$ the WLS slope weight, the WLS weights are linear-unbiased, attain the noise-gain
floor, and no linear-unbiased weight vector has smaller noise gain:

$$
\left(\sum_k \lambda_k = 0 \ \wedge\ \sum_k \lambda_k E_k = 1\right)
\ \wedge\
\left(\sum_k \frac{\lambda_k^2}{w_k} = \frac{1}{S_w} \ \wedge\ \frac{1}{S_w} \le \sum_k \frac{a_k^2}{w_k}\right).
$$

## 2. Physical meaning

In the reduced CF-LIBS reading, a Boltzmann-plot ordinate is
$y_k = \ln(I_k/(g_k A_k))$ against upper-level energy $E_k$. If additive ordinate noise is
uncorrelated and has known variance $\sigma_k^2 = 1/w_k$, then the variance of a linear slope
estimator $\sum_k a_k y_k$ is the noise gain $\sum_k a_k^2/w_k$ [derivation, unchecked]. The
headline theorem is the deterministic core of Aitken's result: among weight vectors satisfying the
two unbiasedness constraints, the WLS weights minimize $\sum_k a_k^2/w_k$, and the minimum is
$1/S_w$. Reading that minimum as a minimum variance (WLS is BLUE) requires the variance identity
above, which is not proved here (see §6).

The `gMean_const_eq_wMean` member connects the weighted mean used in the WLS construction to the
one-group case of the fixed-effects design mean. The `interceptDiff_noiseGain` member gives the
noise gain of the common-slope intercept difference used when comparing two species. In the
reduced reading [derivation, unchecked], that intercept difference estimates
$\ln(FN_a/U_a)-\ln(FN_b/U_b)$; the full abundance ratio also carries the temperature-dependent
partition-function ratio. `Alt.betaHat` is not itself an FT-10 sub-claim: it is the pre-existing
OLS-slope random variable, whose REDUCED model tag was added in PR #8 and which is carried on this
card.

## 4. Proof idea

The headline proof first uses the private helper `wlsWeight_unbiased` to prove that
$\sum_k \lambda_k=0$ and $\sum_k \lambda_k E_k=1$. That helper derives nonemptiness and positive
total weight from H2 and H1 using `Finset.exists_ne_zero_of_sum_ne_zero` and `Finset.sum_pos`,
then cancels the weighted centred sum using `Finset.mul_sum`, `Finset.sum_sub_distrib`,
`Finset.sum_congr`, `div_mul_cancel₀`, `zero_div`, and `div_self`.

The equality part rewrites each term
$\lambda_k^2/w_k$ to $w_k(E_k-\bar E_w)^2/S_w^2$ with `field_simp`, sums with
`Finset.sum_div`, and finishes by `field_simp`. The inequality part first proves
$\sum_k a_k(E_k-\bar E_w)=1$ from H3 and H4, then applies the weighted Cauchy–Schwarz lemma
`Finset.sum_sq_le_sum_mul_sum_of_sq_le_mul` with
$r_k=a_k(E_k-\bar E_w)$, $f_k=a_k^2/w_k$, and $g_k=w_k(E_k-\bar E_w)^2$. Positivity is supplied by
`div_nonneg`, `mul_nonneg`, and `sq_nonneg`; the final division by $S_w$ uses `div_le_iff₀` and
`linarith`.

`gMean_const_eq_wMean` is a definitional reduction by `simp [gMean, wMean]`.
`interceptDiff_noiseGain` expands `idWeightA` and `idWeightB`, uses finite-sum linearity such as
`Finset.sum_sub_distrib`, `Finset.mul_sum`, and `Finset.sum_congr`, and closes linearity by `ring`.
The squared-sum part uses the private helper `sum_sq_affine_centered` (built on
`centered_sum_zero`) for each species, then `field_simp` and `ring`; `Nat.cast_ne_zero` and
`Fintype.card_ne_zero` give nonzero line counts. `Alt.betaHat` is a definition; the companion
result `olsSlope_estimator_eq` uses `olsSlope_eq_centered`, `centered_sum_zero`,
`centered_mul_self`, `Finset.sum_div`, and `div_self` to show that the intercept cancels and the
estimator equals truth plus weighted noise.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** No pipeline code names these declarations. The pipeline's multi-element
  Boltzmann fit `IterativeCFLIBSSolver._fit_common_boltzmann_plane`
  (`cflibs/inversion/solve/iterative.py`) fits one pooled slope with per-element weighted centring
  and inverse-variance weights, which `_cap_boltzmann_weights` then caps. Its one-group case uses
  the headline's WLS weight vector only when the cap does not bind; capped weights are still
  linear-unbiased, so under the reduced noise model their noise gain is at least $1/S_w$
  [derivation, unchecked]. The SB-graph rows built by `_collect_sb_graph_rows` are unit-weighted,
  which is the homoscedastic reading of `interceptDiff_noiseGain`.
- **Proposed use:** Use the headline theorem as the checked deterministic floor for heteroscedastic
  WLS slope weighting. Use `interceptDiff_noiseGain` as the algebraic companion to any future
  variance result for the common-slope intercept difference. Use `Alt.betaHat` as the REDUCED
  model row for OLS-slope random-variable results.
- **What it would change:** It changes the rigor of the pipeline's statistical justification, not
  its measurement accuracy. It replaces an informal “WLS is the best linear unbiased estimator”
  statement with a checked noise-gain floor and exact attainment statement, and it gives a checked
  noise-gain formula for the intercept difference used in ratio extraction.
- **How it would be validated:** Monte Carlo on synthetic Boltzmann plots with known
  heteroscedastic $\sigma_k$ and uncorrelated Gaussian ordinate noise: the empirical variance of
  the WLS slope should match $1/S_w$ (with $w_k=1/\sigma_k^2$), and the capped-weight slope of
  `_fit_common_boltzmann_plane` should show variance at least $1/S_w$. Falsification arm: an
  empirical WLS variance that departs from $1/S_w$ beyond Monte Carlo error would show that the
  noise-model reading (uncorrelated, known $\sigma_k$), not the theorem, is wrong for that
  data-generating process.
