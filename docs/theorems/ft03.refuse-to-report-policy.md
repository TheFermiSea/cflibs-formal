### 1.2 Definitions used

- `pasPolicy` — for weights $w$, losses $l$, bounds $L,U$, price $\lambda$, and a choice $\psi$ (a decidable predicate) on $A$, the policy answers item $i$ when $U_i \le \lambda$, refuses it when $\lambda < L_i$, and otherwise answers exactly when $\psi(i)$ holds. Its weighted value is

$$
\Pi(\psi) = \sum_i w_i \cdot
\begin{cases}
l_i, & U_i \le \lambda \\
\lambda, & \lambda < L_i,\ \lambda < U_i \\
l_i, & i \in A,\ \psi(i) \\
\lambda, & i \in A,\ \neg\psi(i)
\end{cases}
$$

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hw`) — $\forall i,\ 0 \le w_i$.
- **H2** (`hL`) — $\forall i,\ L_i \le l_i$.
- **H3** (`hU`) — $\forall i,\ l_i \le U_i$.

### 1.4 Conclusion

Under H1–H3, writing $\psi \equiv \bot$ for the choice rule "refuse on $A$" and $\psi \equiv \top$ for "answer on $A$", the theorem asserts three inequalities:

(a) Refusing on $A$ costs at most always abstaining:
$$
\Pi(\bot) \le \Bigl(\sum_i w_i\Bigr)\lambda.
$$

(b) Refusing on $A$ has oracle regret at most the weighted gap $\lambda - L$ on $A$:
$$
\Pi(\bot) - \sum_i w_i \min(l_i,\lambda) \;\le\; \sum_{i \in A} w_i\,(\lambda - L_i).
$$

(c) Answering on $A$ costs at most always answering:
$$
\Pi(\top) \le \sum_i w_i\,l_i.
$$

## 2. Physical meaning

Read against the companion's proposed PAS objective, the headline theorem models a priced abstention decision for per-spectrum Aitchison losses [derivation, unchecked]. Each spectrum `i` has a true loss `l i`, a certified lower bound `L i`, and a certified upper bound `U i`. Refusing a spectrum costs the fixed price `lam`; answering costs the true loss. The policy is forced to answer when the certified upper bound is at most `lam`, forced to refuse when the certified lower bound is strictly above `lam`, and makes an explicit choice on the ambiguous set where the certified interval contains `lam`.

The three conjuncts separate two different safe directions. Refusing on the ambiguous set is never worse than refusing everything, and its regret against the `l`-aware oracle is bounded by the total weighted gap `lam - L i` on the ambiguous set. Answering on the ambiguous set is never worse than answering everything, which is the finite-sample form of a nonnegative gate value. The member theorem says that, for strictly positive compositions, the Aitchison distance is bounded by the Euclidean norm of the per-species log-ratio errors after any constant shift `c`. In a LIBS setting, `c` can absorb a common multiplicative factor in all species, to which the centered Aitchison distance is blind [derivation, unchecked].

A one-species composition is not a witness, because its Aitchison distance is identically 0.

## 4. Proof idea

The headline proof unfolds `pasPolicy` and reduces each conjunct to per-item inequalities weighted by `hw` and summed with `Finset.sum_le_sum`. The per-item lemmas used are `item_refuse_le`, `item_refuse_regret`, and `item_answer_le`. The first conjunct also uses `Finset.sum_mul` and `mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_left`; the second uses `Finset.sum_sub_distrib` and `mul_sub`; the third uses `Finset.sum_le_sum` and `mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_left` directly.

The member proof rewrites `aitchisonDist y x` as the square root of a centered log-difference sum using `aitchisonDist_eq_sqrt`. It then rewrites each coordinate difference with `clr_sub_eq_centered_log`, which uses `Real.log_div` and the strict positivity hypotheses. Finally, `sum_sq_centered_le` proves that centering at the mean minimizes the sum of squared deviations, and `Real.sqrt_le_sqrt` gives the displayed inequality. The algebraic closure uses `ring`, `linarith`, `positivity`, and `field_simp`.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** Neither declaration is used by CF-LIBS-improved `main`. `refuse_to_report` in `cflibs/inversion/physics/identifiability.py` shares the name but is an identifiability gate with no loss or price. `selective_score` in `cflibs/evolution/selective_scoring.py` prices refusals at a baseline `lambda` but scores answers by a median selective risk, so conjuncts (a)-(c) do not transfer to it [derivation, unchecked]. The per-spectrum Aitchison PAS with grouped-mean weights is a companion planning objective, not code on `main`.
- **Proposed use:** The headline theorem can serve as the formal guarantee for a priced abstention gate on an Aitchison PAS loss: answer when the certified upper bound is below the abstention price, refuse when the certified lower bound is above it, and expose the ambiguous-set choice. The member theorem can certify an upper bound `U_i` from per-species log-error bounds after removing a common shift [derivation, unchecked].
- **What it would change:** It would change rigor, not accuracy. It would prevent a policy from silently merging two contradictory choices on the ambiguous set, and it would make the regret and gate-value guarantees explicit for weighted finite-sample scoring. It would not claim that LIBS composition estimates become more accurate.
- **How it would be validated:** A pipeline implementation would be falsified if, under the theorem's hypotheses and the same weights, it reported a refuse-on-`A` PAS larger than `(∑ i, w i) * lam`, or a regret larger than the stated ambiguous-set sum, or an answer-on-`A` PAS larger than `∑ i, w i * l i`. For the member certificate, a falsifying example would be strictly positive compositions `x`, `y` and a constant `c` for which `aitchisonDist y x` exceeds the displayed square root.
