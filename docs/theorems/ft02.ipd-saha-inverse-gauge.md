## 1. Statement

Under its physical reading (§2, not proved here), `ipdLogMap` is the log-coordinate form of the
implicit, IPD-aware Saha inverse. Write
$F_{a,b}(\ell) = \log a + b\,e^{\ell/2}$ for `ipdLogMap a b ℓ`. The headline result,
`ipdLogMap_contracts`, is stated first (§1.1–1.5); the three members — the map itself and the
other two theorems — follow in §1.6–1.8, in the order `lean.members` lists them.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hb`) — $0 \le b$.

### 1.4 Conclusion

$$
\exists\,\ell_s \le \ell_1,\ F(\ell_s)=\ell_s \;\wedge\; \bigl(\forall \ell \le \ell_1,\ F(\ell)=\ell \Rightarrow \ell=\ell_s\bigr) \;\wedge\; \bigl(\forall \ell_0 \le \ell_1,\ \lim_n F^{(n)}(\ell_0)=\ell_s\bigr).
$$

In words: on the half-line, $F_{a,b}$ has a fixed point $\ell_s$, it is the only one there, and
Picard iteration from any start in the half-line converges to it.

## 2. Physical meaning

With an ionization-potential depression (IPD) of the assumed form $\Delta\chi = k_BT\cdot b\sqrt{n_e}$
($b \ge 0$ abstract — see Scope), the exact Saha-factor gauge identity $S(\chi-\Delta\chi) =
S(\chi)\cdot e^{\Delta\chi/(k_BT)}$ turns the IPD-aware ratio equation $R\cdot n_e = S(\chi -
\Delta\chi)$ into the implicit equation $n_e = (S(\chi)/R)\cdot e^{b\sqrt{n_e}}$; in the log
coordinate $\ell = \log n_e$, with $a = S(\chi)/R$, this is exactly $\ell = F_{a,b}(\ell)$. **This
physical reading is prose in the Lean module's docstrings, used by no proof in this card**
[derivation, unchecked] — the gauge identity itself (audit item 1) is not a landed declaration
(see §6).

The map's slope $F_{a,b}'(\ell) = b\,e^{\ell/2}/2$ equals $q = \Delta\chi/(2k_BT)$
[derivation, unchecked]: the elasticity of one inner-loop update — the fractional change in the
updated $n_e$ per fractional change in the $n_e$ used to compute the lowering, i.e. half the
lowering in units of $k_BT$. `ipdLogMap_root_subsingleton` says: wherever that local slope
is below $1$ (the "regular" branch), the implicit inverse cannot have two different roots there —
at most one physical $n_e$ solves the IPD-aware equation on that branch. It asserts nothing about
existence; the docstring notes (as motivation, not a proved claim) that a second root, if any,
lies beyond the fold ($q \ge 1$), and the two merge at $q=1$ — this module does not formalize that
bifurcation. `ipdLogMap_contracts` says: if the iteration is known to stay inside a half-line
where the slope never exceeds a fixed $q<1$, repeatedly applying $F_{a,b}$ (Picard iteration is
what the pipeline runs; the scalar equation also has a Lambert-W closed form on the principal
branch, which this module does not formalize) converges to that half-line's unique fixed point
(convergence only; a geometric rate follows from the Banach argument inside the proof but is not
part of the statement, see §3) — gives a sufficient condition for an iterative IPD inner loop to
converge, *provided* the invariance and rate hypotheses actually hold for the iteration being run
(see §5). `ipdInverse_twoPoint_sensitivity` quantifies the inverse's local gain: a fractional
change in the data $a$ (physically, $\propto 1/R$) moves the recovered $\ell = \log n_e$ by at
least the same amount and by at most a factor $1/(1-q)$ more, so the proved upper bound $1/(1-q)$
on the sensitivity grows without bound as $q \to 1$; that the actual sensitivity diverges at the
fold is the Lean docstring's remark, not a proved claim here.

  - It does **not** claim the physical reading itself — that $a = S(\chi)/R$, that
    $\Delta\chi = k_BT\cdot b\sqrt{n_e}$, or that any specific IPD law (Debye–Hückel or
    otherwise, D18's DH-ei choice included) is the correct lowering — that binding (FT-02 item 8)
    is not written in `CflibsFormal/` under this or any other card.
  - It does **not** claim existence of a fixed point in general: `ipdLogMap_root_subsingleton`
    proves uniqueness only (the docstring is explicit that for `0 < b` and large `a` there is no
    root at all), and `ipdLogMap_contracts`'s existence clause is conditional on `hmaps` already
    holding, which is not automatic from `hb`/`hq`/`hq1` alone.
  - It does **not** claim that CF-LIBS-improved's actual inner IPD loop (`_ne_for_T`, §5) is
    inside an `hmaps`-invariant half-line or satisfies `q < 1` at any real spectrum — no proof or
    runtime check here connects the Lean hypotheses to that Python loop's three fixed iterations.
  - It does **not** claim or state any rate or error bound for a fixed, finite number of
    iterations (e.g. no "`q³`" bound appears in any hypothesis or conclusion here); such a bound
    is a corollary a reader would have to derive separately from the Lipschitz constant used
    inside the proof, not something these four declarations assert.
  - It does **not** claim the gauge identity $S(\chi-d) = S(\chi)\cdot e^{d/(k_BT)}$ or the
    resulting mismatch/composition-invariance corollaries sketched under FT-02 items 1–2 — those
    are not landed declarations (§6).
  - It does **not** claim anything about `ipdInverse_twoPoint_sensitivity`'s `l1`, `l2` beyond
    what the hypotheses hand it: they are taken as given fixed points (`h1`, `h2`), not asserted
    to exist or to be the limits any Picard iteration converges to; the statement is a
    finite-difference bound between two given fixed points, not (as in the audit's informal
    write-up) a claim about $d\log n_e/d\log R$.
  - It does **not** claim that $q$ in `ipdLogMap_contracts` is the slope at the fixed point
    $\ell_s$ itself — it is an upper bound on the slope over the whole half-line
    $(-\infty,\ell_1]$, which need not be attained at $\ell_s$.
  - It does **not** claim anything that holds across a change of temperature $T$: both `a` and
    `b` are themselves functions of $T$ in the physical reading, and no declaration in this
    family relates two different $T$'s.
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:**
  - Non-vacuity for `ipdLogMap_root_subsingleton`: `CflibsFormal/IpdSahaInverse.lean` contains two
    `example`s, already compiled as part of this module and restated here as named,
    axiom-checked theorems [scratch: evidence/ft02.ipd-saha-inverse-gauge/check.lean], showing
    $a = e^{-1/2}$, $b = 1/2$, $\ell = 0$ is a genuine member of $R_{a,b}$ (not a vacuous
    statement about an empty set), and that the theorem then pins every member of $R_{a,b}$ at
    that instance to $0$.
  - Non-vacuity for the headline `ipdLogMap_contracts`: at $a = e^{-1/2}$, $b = 1/2$,
    $\ell_1 = 0$, $q = 1/4$, the half-line $(-\infty, 0]$ is `hmaps`-invariant and the theorem's
    existence, uniqueness and convergence conclusions all hold simultaneously
    [scratch: evidence/ft02.ipd-saha-inverse-gauge/check.lean] — nothing here shows the theorem
    vacuous.
  - Sharpness of `ha1 : 0 < a1` and `hb : 0 ≤ b` in `ipdInverse_twoPoint_sensitivity`: the
    2026-09-24 audit's verifier recorded two falsifying instances for the unguarded sketch — one
    with $a_1 < 0$ (so `Real.log a1` is a junk value), one with $b < 0$ — each satisfying every
    *other* hypothesis (`hq`, `h1`, `h2`, `ha`, `hreg`) while the conclusion's lower bound fails.
    Independently re-derived by plain arithmetic (not the audit's own script) at
    [numerics: evidence/ft02.ipd-saha-inverse-gauge/numerics.py]: both instances do falsify the
    unguarded lower bound, confirming `ha1` and `hb` are load-bearing, not decorative. The landed
    `ipdInverse_twoPoint_sensitivity` carries both guards and is not falsified by either instance.
  - Sharpness of `q < 1`: not independently re-derived here as a bifurcation/fold theorem (no such
    theorem exists in this module — see "What it does NOT claim"); §2's "fold at $q=1$" remark is
    descriptive motivation from the Lean docstring, not a claim this card makes on its own
    authority.

## 4. Proof idea

All three theorems share two derivative-free real-analysis lemmas: the private `exp_half_sub_le`
(from `Real.add_one_le_exp`, the tangent-line-below-`exp` bound; a chord bound,
$e^{y/2}-e^{x/2} \le \tfrac{e^{y/2}}{2}(y-x)$ for all reals) and `exp_half_mono` (from
`Real.exp_le_exp`; monotonicity of $e^{\cdot/2}$). No derivative (`HasDerivAt`) is ever taken; the
MVT-style bound is obtained algebraically from `Real.add_one_le_exp` instead.

- **`ipdLogMap_root_subsingleton`.** The private lemma `no_two_roots` takes two candidate roots
  `x < y` with `y` regular and derives `False`, splitting on the sign of `b`
  (`le_or_gt b 0`): for `b ≤ 0` the fixed-point equation forces `y - x ≤ 0`, contradicting
  `x < y` directly (`mul_nonpos_of_nonpos_of_nonneg`); for `b > 0` the chord bound plus the
  regular-branch hypothesis force `y - x < y - x` (`mul_lt_mul_of_pos_right`, then `nlinarith`).
  The public theorem applies `no_two_roots` to both orderings of an arbitrary pair `x`, `y` in the
  set (`lt_or_gt_of_ne`).
- **`ipdLogMap_contracts`.** The private `ipdLogMap_lipschitz_Iic` bounds
  `|ipdLogMap a b x - ipdLogMap a b y| ≤ q * |x - y|` on `Set.Iic ℓ1` via the chord bound. The
  main proof builds `K : NNReal := ⟨q, hq0⟩`, shows `ContractingWith K` for `ipdLogMap a b`
  restricted to the complete subtype `Set.Iic ℓ1` (`isClosed_Iic.isComplete`), and invokes
  mathlib's `ContractingWith.exists_fixedPoint'` **twice**: once at `x = ℓ1` (to get existence),
  once at an arbitrary `ℓ0` (for the convergence clause) — discarding that lemma's
  convergence-from-ℓ1 and rate outputs (`⟨ℓs, hℓs, hfixpt, -, -⟩`) and instead proving uniqueness
  (`huniq`) directly from the Lipschitz bound by contradiction (`nlinarith`), then reusing `huniq`
  to identify the second call's fixed point with the first's.
- **`ipdInverse_twoPoint_sensitivity`.** The private `ipdLogMap_fixed_iff` rearranges the
  fixed-point equations to `l - b*exp(l/2) = log a`. The proof first shows `l1 ≤ l2` by
  contradiction (assuming `l2 < l1`, rewriting `hreg` at `max l1 l2 = l1`, then combining the
  chord bound with `Real.log_le_log` via `nlinarith`), then rewrites `max l1 l2 = l2` and derives
  the lower bound from `exp_half_mono` and `hb`, the upper bound from the chord bound, `hreg` and
  `le_div_iff₀`.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state.** CF-LIBS-improved's joint least-squares solver contains a nested routine,
  `_ne_for_T` inside `joint_wls_solve` (`cflibs/jitpipe/solve.py`, at
  `main@b142ae25b5e4cd1e15b76d0c4f08b328e400e1c0`), whose own docstring calls it a "Tiered `n_e`
  at the running `T`" estimate. On its `sb_offset` tier it restarts from the pinned n_e (Stark or
  pressure-balance) and runs a *fixed* three-step Picard-shaped loop, per element exactly of the
  form `ipdLogMap` encodes (the median across elements preserves it while the accepted set, the
  acceptance window and the ip_eff ≥ 0 clamp are unchanged), but with an electron-only Debye
  length rather than D18's chosen DH-ei form, and with no code that checks `hmaps` (that the
  loop's own starting bound stays invariant) or the slope bound `q < 1` at runtime. No identifier
  in that file names `ipdLogMap`, `IpdSahaInverse` or FT-02 [pipeline anchor: see front matter
  `pipeline[]` for the exact path/symbol/relation].
- **Proposed use.** Expose the realized per-step slope
  $q_{\text{run}} = b\,e^{\ell/2}/2$ and the loop's own half-line bound as values the caller can
  inspect, so `ipdLogMap_contracts`'s hypotheses (`hmaps`, `q < 1`) become a *checkable*
  precondition on each call instead of an unstated assumption baked into "always run exactly
  three steps."
- **What it would change.** Nothing about measurement accuracy — a call that already satisfies
  the regime would return the same numeric `n_e` it does today. What would change is that the
  pipeline would carry a checkable certificate that the iteration it ran was actually inside the
  regime `ipdLogMap_contracts` proves converges (or an explicit flag when it is not), replacing an
  unstated assumption with a checked one. Per `AGENTS.md`: this is a rigor improvement, not an
  accuracy claim, and is not pitched as one.
- **How it would be validated.** Instrument `_ne_for_T` (or a Python mirror of `ipdLogMap`) to log
  $q_{\text{run}}$ at each of its three steps and whether the running n_e estimate sequence stays
  inside the caller's own declared bound. **Falsification arm:** on synthetic $(T,R,\chi)$ inputs
  swept toward the fold, whenever the proposed check reports that the start lies in the half-line,
  $F(\ell_1) \le \ell_1$ and $q_{\text{run}} \le q < 1$, a many-step (e.g. 50-step) reference
  iteration must converge, and to the same fixed point the three-step loop approaches; any
  passing-check input where it does not falsifies the check (or this card's reading of the
  hypotheses). Inputs where the check fails yet the loop converges are expected — the hypotheses
  are sufficient, not necessary — and do not count against it. The fold density
  ($\approx 9.08\times10^{19}$ cm$^{-3}$ at 11 kK, electron-only Debye length
  [numerics: evidence/ft02.ipd-saha-inverse-gauge/numerics.py]) depends on the lowering law and
  sits near the loop's $10^{20}$ cm$^{-3}$ acceptance window.
