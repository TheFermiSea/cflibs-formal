### 1.2 Definitions used

- `mean` — for a finite index type, $\bar f = \frac{1}{|\iota|}\sum_k f_k$.
- `olsWeight` — $w_k = (E_k - \bar E)/\sum_j (E_j - \bar E)^2$; the denominator is strictly positive under H1.
- `betaHat` — the OLS slope $\hat\beta$ of the realized points $(E_k, \alpha + \beta E_k + \varepsilon_k(\omega))$.
- `iIndepFun` — mutual independence of the family $(\varepsilon_k)_{k\in\iota}$ with respect to $\mu$.
- `HasSubgaussianMGF` — a real random variable $X$ has variance proxy $c\ge0$ if, for every real
  $t$, $e^{tX}$ is $\mu$-integrable and $\int e^{tX}\,d\mu\le\exp(c\,t^2/2)$ (mathlib fields
  `integrable_exp_mul`, `mgf_le`).
- `olsIntercept` — for a response vector $y$, $\hat\alpha = \bar y - \hat\beta\,\bar E$.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1** (`hvar`) — the energy spread is strictly positive: $0 \lt \sum_k (E_k - \bar E)^2$.
- **H2** (`hδ`) — the threshold is nonnegative: $0 \le \delta$.
- **H3** (`hindep`) — the noises $(\varepsilon_k)_{k\in\iota}$ are mutually independent under $\mu$.
- **H4** (`hsubG`) — for every $k$, $\varepsilon_k$ is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy $c_k$ in the
  sense of §1.2.

### 1.4 Conclusion

Under H1–H4, the probability that the OLS slope misses the true slope by at least $\delta$ is
bounded by

$$
\mu\{\omega:\ \delta\le|\hat\beta(\omega)-\beta|\}\ \le\ 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\delta^2}{2\sum_k w_k^2 c_k}\right).
$$

## 2. Physical meaning

The model is the idealized linear Boltzmann plot $y_k = \alpha + \beta E_k + \varepsilon_k$ for log-intensity ordinates [derivation, unchecked]. In a CF-LIBS reading, $\beta=-1/(k_BT)$ is the
Boltzmann-plot slope (the repository's temperature reader `Alt.temp_slope_event_subset` works on
the sign-normalized branch $1/(k_BT)\gt0$, i.e. on $-\beta$), and $\alpha$ carries concentration
information [derivation, unchecked]. The theorem says that, if the log-ordinate noises are
independent and sub-Gaussian with line-specific proxies $c_k$, the OLS slope error decays like a
sub-Gaussian tail whose scale is $\sum_k w_k^2 c_k$.

A common physics reading is $c_k \approx 1/\mathrm{SNR}_k^2$ by a first-order delta-method approximation on $\log I$ [derivation, unchecked]. That approximation is not part of the Lean statement. With $c_k\equiv c$ the bound becomes $2\exp(-\delta^2 S_E/(2c))$, the homoscedastic twin
[proved: CflibsFormal.Alt.olsSlope_subGaussian_tail].

## 3. Scope and validity

The generated Facts block records the scope tags. The curated statement is a probability inequality over the OLS Boltzmann-plot model behind `Alt.betaHat`; the physics reading is approximate.

- **Idealizations encoded:** finite nonempty set of lines; exactly known energies; additive noise on the log ordinate only; independent sub-Gaussian noises; known nonnegative proxies; no optical-thickness, non-LTE, or multi-zone complications; zero-mean noise: H4 under the probability
  measure of H3 forces $\mathbb E[\varepsilon_k]=0$ [derivation, unchecked], so systematic ordinate
  errors (atomic-data $gA$ errors, self-absorption, calibration drift) are outside the bound; the
  estimator is the unweighted OLS slope.
- **What it does NOT claim:** It does not claim that measured log-intensity noise is independent or sub-Gaussian. It does not claim $c_k = 1/\mathrm{SNR}_k^2$ or any SNR calibration. It does not claim the exact Gaussian law of $\hat\beta$, finite-sample coverage for unknown variances, or a temperature-error bound. It does not say the pipeline gate uses this bound, and it does not improve measurement accuracy. It does not bound the total slope error including systematic bias. It
  does not claim that a delta-method $1/\mathrm{SNR}_k^2$ is a valid sub-Gaussian proxy: a proxy
  must dominate the variance, and $\log I$ is not sub-Gaussian when $I$ can approach $0$. It does
  not bound the inverse-variance-weighted or multi-element common-slope estimator. It proves no
  tail bound for $\hat\alpha$ (only the Chebyshev intercept tail `Alt.alphaHat_chebyshev` exists).
- **Hypothesis sharpness / non-vacuity:** The archived statement audit records a non-vacuous witness: for $E=(0,1,2)$, $c=(1,2,3)$, and $\delta=3$, the proxy sum is $1$ and the right-hand side is $2e^{-9/2} \lt 1$; zero noise under a Dirac measure satisfies H3 and H4 [derivation, unchecked].
  H1 is needed by the proof route (`olsSlope_estimator_eq`) but not for truth: when
  $\sum_k(E_k-\bar E)^2=0$, every $w_k=0$ and the right side is $2\ge1$. H2 is sharp: for
  $\delta\lt0$ the left side is $1$, while the right side falls below $1$ for large $|\delta|$
  [derivation, unchecked] (archived statement audit
  `docs/archive/openprover/staging/2026-09-24/_statement-audit/ft12_prob.lean`, rc=0; H3 for the
  zero-noise family is [derivation, unchecked]).

## 4. Proof idea

For every $\omega$, $\hat\beta(\omega)=\beta+\sum_k w_k\varepsilon_k(\omega)$
[proved: CflibsFormal.Alt.olsSlope_estimator_eq]. The proof rewrites `betaHat` via this identity,
then applies `iIndepFun.comp` to the weighted noises, uses `HasSubgaussianMGF.const_mul` to assign proxy `w_k^2 c_k` to each weighted noise, and combines them with `HasSubgaussianMGF.sum_of_iIndepFun`. The one-sided bounds come from `HasSubgaussianMGF.measure_ge_le` and its negation. The two-sided event is split with `le_abs`, and the union is bounded by `measureReal_union_le`. Coercions are handled by `NNReal.coe_sum` and `NNReal.coe_mul`. No positivity hypothesis on `c` is needed because the sub-Gaussian tail bound and Lean's total division make the zero-proxy case trivial.

## 5. Role in the composition-extraction pipeline

- **Current state:** The theorem is landed in `CflibsFormal/Alt/StochasticBudget.lean`, and
  nothing in CF-LIBS-improved names it. The C4 slope-budget certificate
  (`slope_budget_certificate`, `cflibs/inversion/physics/certificates.py`) is a deterministic
  predicate. The shipped certificate wiring (`cflibs/inversion/physics/certificates_wiring.py`)
  skips C4. Only the opt-in evaluator-side gate of the algorithm-search harness
  (`hard_certificate_gate`, `cflibs/evolution/certificate_gate.py`) evaluates C4 as HARD. It feeds
  the certificate the one-sigma per-line error `VerifiedWitness.eps`, the reciprocal of the
  smallest `VerifiedLine.effective_snr` over the witness lines, and treats that scale as a per-line
  bound. This theorem is the building block for replacing that premise with a stated tail
  probability.
- **Proposed use:** A future `slopeTailCert` could assert $2\exp(-\tau_\beta^2/(2\sum_k w_k^2c_k))\le p$ for a risk level $p$; applying this theorem at $\delta=\tau_\beta$ then bounds $\mu\{|\hat\beta-\beta|\ge\tau_\beta\}$ by $p$. A gate that checks several certificates must union-bound their risk levels (FT-12 audit hypothesis note). The candidate certificate would sit under C15, with per-line proxies supplied by measured SNR estimates [derivation, unchecked].
- **What it would change:** It would add a probabilistic rigor layer: a stated tail probability for the slope under stated independence and sub-Gaussianity assumptions, rather than a deterministic one-sigma per-line premise. It would not improve measurement accuracy.
- **How it would be validated:** On repeated-shot spectra of a reference sample, the empirical
  frequency of $|\hat\beta-\beta_{\rm ref}|\ge\tau_\beta$ exceeding the certified $p$ beyond
  sampling error falsifies the applicability of H3/H4 or of the SNR-to-proxy binding, not the
  theorem.
